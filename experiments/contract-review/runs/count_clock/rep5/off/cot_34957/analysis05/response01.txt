The key distinguishing scenario for this counter is the **11:59:59 AM → 12:00:00 PM rollover combined with a mid-row enable deassertion**, because it simultaneously exercises the hour wrap, the AM/PM flip, the BCD carry chain, and the input-timing rule that a mid-row change must not retroactively affect the already-sampled edge.

Consider a row where the pre-edge state is `hh=0x11, mm=0x59, ss=0x59, pm=0` (11:59:59 AM) and the driven inputs are `reset=0, ena=1`. At the posedge, seconds wrap 59→00, minutes wrap 59→00, hours advance 11→12, and because the hour transition is 11→12 the `pm` bit flips from 0 to 1. Expected post-edge outputs: `hh=0x12, mm=0x00, ss=0x00, pm=1`. This is the canonical "noon" transition and is the single most informative row for the checker, since a naive implementation that increments hours modulo 12 without tracking the 11→12 boundary will produce `hh=0x12` but leave `pm=0`, and an implementation that flips `pm` on every hour change will flip it on 12→01 as well (which is correct) but also incorrectly on 01→02 (which is not).

Now suppose a mid-row change after this sample sets `ena=0` before the next edge. The next row's effective input is `ena=0`, so the state holds at PM 12:00:00. The mid-change must not undo the increment already committed at the previous posedge. A checker that applies mid-changes to the *current* row's expected outputs, rather than carrying them forward, would wrongly expect the previous row to have held at 11:59:59 AM — a false failure. Conversely, a checker that ignores mid-changes entirely would wrongly expect the next row to increment again to 12:00:01 PM — also a false failure. The persistent pending-inputs model is what distinguishes these.

The reset priority matters here too: if a mid-row change asserts `reset=1` after the noon sample, the next posedge must force `hh=0x12, mm=0x00, ss=0x00, pm=0` (12:00:00 AM), overriding the held `ena=0`. Reset wins over enable, and reset is synchronous — it takes effect only at the next posedge, not at the moment of assertion.

For the final checker, the useful discipline is: treat each row as (carry pending inputs → apply driven inputs → sample with reset-then-enable priority → compare), keep the pending-inputs register persistent across rows, and validate that `pm` flips exactly on the 11→12 and 12→01 hour transitions and nowhere else. Also validate BCD nibbles (each ≤ 9) and the hour range 01–12, since a wrap bug often manifests as `hh=0x00` or `hh=0x13` rather than a wrong `pm`.
