One implementation mistake worth isolating for the final checker is conflating the *hour digit* with the *hour value* when deciding the AM/PM flip. The spec says hours are two BCD digits for 01–12, and `pm` is asserted iff the clock is PM. The natural boundary is therefore the 11→12 transition (AM→PM) and the 12→01 transition (PM→AM). A common bug is to compute the next hour as `(hour_value % 12) + 1` and then set `pm` from the *new* hour value being ≥ 12, or to flip `pm` whenever the hour digit changes. Both look plausible and both fail on specific rows.

Consider the noon row: pre-edge state is 11:59:59 AM, driven `reset=0, ena=1`. Correct post-edge is `hh=0x12, mm=0x00, ss=0x00, pm=1`. An implementation that flips `pm` only when the *tens* digit of the hour changes will see 11→12 (tens 1→1) and leave `pm=0` — wrong. An implementation that flips `pm` on every hour increment will get noon right but then flip again on 12→01, which is actually correct, yet also flip on 01→02, which is wrong. So the checker cannot rely on "pm flips on hour change" as a proxy; it must verify `pm` against the actual 12-hour semantics at every row, especially the two boundary transitions and the non-boundary 01→02, 02→03, …, 10→11 rows where `pm` must hold.

A second, subtler mistake is treating reset as asynchronous or as lower priority than enable. The spec is explicit: reset is active-high *synchronous*, has higher priority than enable, and can occur even when not enabled. A checker that models reset as clearing state immediately on assertion (before the posedge) will mispredict the row where `reset` is asserted mid-cycle: the state must remain at its pre-edge value until the posedge, then jump to 12:00:00 AM. Likewise, a checker that lets `ena=1` override a simultaneous `reset=1` will expect an increment instead of the reset value. The correct evaluation order at each posedge is: if `reset` then load 12:00:00 AM (`hh=0x12, mm=0x00, ss=0x00, pm=0`); else if `ena` then advance; else hold.

For the final checker, the useful discipline is to keep a persistent pending-inputs register, apply driven inputs, then evaluate reset-before-enable at the posedge, and compare all four outputs. Validate BCD nibbles (each ≤ 9) and hour range 01–12 independently of `pm`, because wrap bugs frequently surface as `hh=0x00` or `hh=0x13` rather than as a wrong `pm` bit. Finally, assert `pm` explicitly on the 11→12 and 12→01 rows and assert it *unchanged* on the other hour-increment rows; that pair of positive and negative checks catches both the missed-flip and the over-flip variants.
