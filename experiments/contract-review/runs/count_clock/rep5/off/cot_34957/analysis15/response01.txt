The public specification describes a 12-hour clock counter with BCD outputs and an AM/PM indicator, driven by a fast `clk` and a once-per-second enable pulse `ena`. The key design point is that `reset` is synchronous, active-high, and has higher priority than `ena`; it forces the clock to `12:00:00 AM` regardless of whether an increment was otherwise due. The checker must therefore treat reset as an unconditional first branch at every sampled posedge, before any enable or carry logic is considered.

State consists of the BCD hours `hh` (01–12), minutes `mm` (00–59), seconds `ss` (00–59), and the `pm` flag. On a sampled posedge with `reset=1`, the next state is the constant `hh=0x12, mm=0x00, ss=0x00, pm=0`, independent of `ena` and independent of the current state. On a sampled posedge with `reset=0` and `ena=0`, all outputs hold. On a sampled posedge with `reset=0` and `ena=1`, seconds increment in BCD; if seconds were `59`, they wrap to `00` and minutes increment; if minutes were also `59`, they wrap to `00` and hours advance; if hours were `12`, they wrap to `01` and `pm` toggles; if hours were `11`, they advance to `12` and `pm` toggles; otherwise hours advance without a `pm` change. The `pm` flag flips on exactly the two hour transitions `11→12` and `12→01`, and holds on every other enabled increment and on every disabled increment.

The timing protocol matters for the checker. Inputs are driven before the sampled edge; mid-cycle input changes occur after sampling and before the other edge, and are carried to the start of the next row. The checker should snapshot driven inputs at each posedge, evaluate the transition from that snapshot, compare outputs, and only then fold mid-cycle changes into the pending-input register for the next row. A post-edge reset must not retroactively alter the row already sampled. Power-up state is unspecified until the first sampled `reset=1` edge establishes `12:00:00 AM`; earlier rows should not be compared.

The sharpest single test is the collision `reset=1, ena=1` at `11:59:59 AM`. An enable-first model would increment seconds `59→00`, minutes `59→00`, hours `11→12`, and toggle `pm` from AM to PM, producing `12:00:00 PM`. The correct sampled result is `12:00:00 AM` with `pm=0`, because reset suppresses the entire increment, including the hour carry and the `pm` flip. This one row distinguishes correct reset-priority logic from an enable-first model and simultaneously guards the `pm` transition that a range-only checker would overlook.

The final checker should combine structural and semantic invariants. Structurally, `hh`, `mm`, and `ss` must be valid BCD in range: hours `01`–`12`, minutes and seconds `00`–`59`. Semantically, `pm` must flip on exactly `11→12` and `12→01` and hold elsewhere. A range-only checker can miss a stuck or inverted `pm`; a `pm`-only checker can miss malformed BCD such as `0x13` or `0x00`. Both classes are needed, and the reset branch must dominate them. The highest-value focus is thus an unconditional, first-priority reset branch at every posedge, paired with BCD/range invariants and explicit `pm` flip checks on the two hour transitions.
