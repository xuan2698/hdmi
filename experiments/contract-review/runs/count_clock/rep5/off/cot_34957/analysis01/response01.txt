The public spec fixes a synchronous 12-hour clock with BCD outputs and an AM/PM flag. The key timing question for the checker is not the arithmetic of counting but *when* inputs are considered valid relative to `clk`. The spec says the counters are "clocked by a fast-running clk, with a pulse on ena whenever your clock should increment." That makes `ena` a synchronous enable sampled at the active clock edge, not an asynchronous event. Likewise `reset` is explicitly "active high synchronous" and has higher priority than `ena`, and it "can occur even when not enabled." So at every rising edge of `clk`, the checker should evaluate `reset` first, then `ena`, then the current state, and only then update `pm`, `hh`, `mm`, `ss`.

The phrase "sample after the posedge edge has settled" tells us the checker reads outputs after the edge's effects have propagated, i.e., it observes the post-edge state. "Each row's values are driven before the sampled edge" means the stimulus for a row is applied before the edge that samples it; the checker must not treat a value that changes at the same instant as the edge as belonging to the previous row. In practice this means the testbench should drive inputs, allow them to settle, then take the sample at the posedge, and compare against the expected post-edge outputs.

The "optional mid input changes occur AFTER sampling, before the other edge, and are carried to the start of the next row" clause is the subtle one. A mid-row change is not sampled by the current edge; it persists and becomes the input for the next sampled edge. So the checker's model should be: for row *i*, the effective inputs are whatever was driven before that row's sampled edge, including any mid-change carried over from row *i-1*. A mid-change must never retroactively alter row *i*'s expected outputs. This matters most for `ena` and `reset`: if `reset` is asserted mid-row, it does not reset the clock until the next posedge; if `ena` pulses mid-row, the increment happens at the next posedge, not immediately.

Priority ordering also needs care. Because `reset` is synchronous and higher priority than `ena`, a row with both `reset=1` and `ena=1` must produce the reset state "12:00 AM" (`hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=0`), not an increment. A row with `reset=0, ena=0` must hold all outputs, including `pm`. A row with `reset=0, ena=1` advances seconds, with BCD carry into minutes and hours, and the 12-hour wrap: after 11:59:59 AM comes 12:00:00 PM, and after 11:59:59 PM comes 12:00:00 AM with `pm` toggling appropriately. Note the hour range is 01–12, so `hh` never takes 00; the reset value is 12, and the wrap from 12 goes to 01 (with AM/PM flip), not to 00.

Worked example: suppose row *i* is sampled with `reset=0, ena=1` and state 11:59:59 AM. Expected post-edge: `hh=0x12, mm=0x00, ss=0x00, pm=1`. If a mid-change then sets `ena=0` before row *i+1*'s edge, row *i+1* holds that PM 12:00:00 state; the mid-change does not undo the increment already sampled.

For the final Python checker, the useful point is to separate three phases per row: (1) apply carried mid-changes and row inputs, (2) sample at posedge using reset-then-ena priority, (3) compute expected post-edge outputs and compare. Keep a persistent "pending inputs" variable so mid-changes are carried forward, and never let a mid-change affect the row already sampled. Also verify BCD digit validity (each nibble 0–9, hours 01–12) and that `pm` only flips on the 11→12 or 12→1 hour transitions, not on every hour.
