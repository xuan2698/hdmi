The key distinguishing case for this 12-hour clock is the interaction between reset priority and the `pm` toggle at the 11↔12 boundary. Consider a sampled state of `11:59:59 AM` with `reset=1` and `ena=1` simultaneously. A naive model that applies the enable increment first would roll seconds to `00`, minutes to `00`, hours to `12`, and toggle `pm` to PM, producing `12:00:00 PM`. The correct behavior is `12:00:00 AM` with `pm=0`, because synchronous reset has priority over enable and suppresses the entire increment — including the hour carry and the `pm` flip. This single row separates reset-priority logic from enable-first logic, and it also exercises the `pm` transition that a range-only checker would miss.

For the final Python checker, the most useful focus is an unconditional first-priority reset branch evaluated at every sampled posedge, combined with two independent invariant classes: BCD/range validity for all three fields, and explicit `pm` flip checks on exactly `11→12` and `12→01` with holds elsewhere. Range-only checking can miss a stuck or inverted `pm`; `pm`-only checking can miss malformed BCD such as `0x13` or `0x00`. Both are needed, and reset must dominate both.

The timing protocol is the other half of the specification. Inputs are driven before the sampled edge; mid-cycle changes occur after sampling and before the opposite edge, and are carried into the next row. The checker should therefore snapshot driven inputs at each posedge, compute the transition from that snapshot, compare outputs, and only afterward merge mid-cycle changes into the pending-input state for the following row. A reset asserted after a sampled edge must not retroactively rewrite the row already evaluated. Power-up state is unspecified until the first sampled `reset=1` edge establishes `12:00:00 AM`; rows before that point should be skipped rather than compared.

The `pm` toggle rule deserves emphasis because it is easy to get subtly wrong. `pm` flips only on the enabled transition from `11:59:59` to `12:00:00` (AM→PM) and on the enabled transition from `12:59:59` to `01:00:00` (PM→AM). Every other enabled increment holds `pm`, and disabled increments hold all outputs including `pm`. Reset forces `pm=0`. A checker that only validates numeric ranges will accept a clock whose `pm` bit is stuck or inverted, so the explicit flip-and-hold assertions are essential. Conversely, a checker that only watches `pm` will accept malformed BCD like `0x13` in the seconds field, so BCD digit validation (each nibble ≤ 9, and field ranges 01–12, 00–59, 00–59) must run independently. Reset must dominate both classes: when the sampled `reset` is high, the expected outputs are fixed at `12:00:00 AM` regardless of `ena` or the prior state, and no range or `pm` assertion should be evaluated against the pre-reset values.
