The reset behavior is the single most important semantic anchor in this design, and the final checker should treat it as such. Reset is synchronous, active high, and has priority over enable. That means at any sampled posedge where `reset=1`, the entire state must become `12:00:00 AM` with `pm=0`, regardless of `ena`, regardless of the current time, and regardless of any carry that would otherwise have propagated. The checker should model this as an unconditional first branch evaluated at every posedge, before any enable or carry logic is considered. If reset is asserted, the expected next state is the constant reset state; nothing else in the row matters.

This priority ordering is not a cosmetic detail. Consider the collision case `11:59:59 AM` with both `reset=1` and `ena=1`. An enable-first model would increment seconds `59→00`, minutes `59→00`, hours `11→12`, and toggle `pm` from AM to PM, producing `12:00:00 PM`. That is wrong. The correct sampled result is `12:00:00 AM` with `pm=0`, because reset suppresses the entire increment, including the hour carry and the `pm` flip. The final checker should therefore evaluate reset as an unconditional first branch at every sampled posedge, before any enable or carry logic, and compare against the reset constant.

This example also shows why the checker needs both structural and semantic invariants. Structurally, `hh`, `mm`, and `ss` must be valid BCD in range: hours `01`–`12`, minutes and seconds `00`–`59`. Semantically, `pm` must flip on exactly the two hour transitions `11→12` and `12→01`, and must hold on every other enabled increment and on every disabled increment. A range-only checker can miss a stuck or inverted `pm`; a `pm`-only checker can miss malformed BCD such as `0x13` or `0x00`. Both classes are needed, and the reset branch must dominate them.

The timing protocol adds a second trap. Mid-cycle input changes occur after sampling and before the other edge, and are carried to the start of the next row. The checker should snapshot driven inputs at each posedge, evaluate the transition from that snapshot, compare outputs, and only then fold mid-cycle changes into the pending-input register for the next row. A post-edge reset must not retroactively alter the row already sampled. Likewise, power-up state is unspecified until the first sampled `reset=1` edge establishes `12:00:00 AM`; the checker should not compare outputs on earlier rows.

The highest-value focus for the final checker is thus an unconditional, first-priority reset branch at every posedge, paired with BCD/range invariants and explicit `pm` flip checks on `11→12` and `12→01`. The `reset=1, ena=1` collision at `11:59:59 AM` is the sharpest single test: it distinguishes correct reset-priority logic from an enable-first model in one row, and it simultaneously guards the `pm` transition that a range-only checker would overlook.
