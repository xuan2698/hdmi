The public interface fixes all widths unambiguously: `reset` and `ena` are 1-bit inputs, `clk` is the sampling clock, and the four outputs are `pm` (1 bit), `hh` (8 bits), `mm` (8 bits), and `ss` (8 bits). The 8-bit widths for `hh`, `mm`, and `ss` are exactly what two BCD digits require—four bits per digit, eight bits per field—so no padding or sign bit is implied. Each field is a packed pair of nibbles, and the checker must treat the high nibble as the tens digit and the low nibble as the units digit. Legal encodings are therefore not the full 0–255 byte range but a sparse subset: `hh` in `0x01`–`0x12`, `mm` in `0x00`–`0x59`, and `ss` in `0x00`–`0x59`, with each nibble individually constrained to 0–9. Values such as `0x0A`, `0x13`, `0x60`, or `0x00` for hours are outside the specified domain even though they fit in eight bits.

Boundary behavior follows from those ranges. Seconds and minutes roll `59→00` and carry into the next field; hours roll within the 12-hour cycle rather than a 24-hour one. The two hour boundaries are `11→12` and `12→01`, and these are precisely the two transitions on which `pm` toggles. Every other enabled increment holds `pm`, and disabled increments hold all outputs including `pm`. Reset is synchronous, active high, and has priority over `ena`, so a sampled `reset=1` forces `12:00:00 AM` regardless of the simultaneous enable value. Because reset can occur while disabled, the checker cannot gate the reset branch on `ena`.

The timing protocol is the other half of the specification. Inputs are driven before the sampled edge; mid-cycle changes occur after sampling and before the opposite edge, and are carried into the next row. The checker should therefore snapshot driven inputs at each posedge, compute the transition from that snapshot, compare outputs, and only afterward merge mid-cycle changes into the pending-input state for the following row. A reset asserted after a sampled edge must not retroactively rewrite the row already evaluated. Power-up state is unspecified until the first sampled `reset=1` edge establishes `12:00:00 AM`; rows before that point should be skipped rather than compared.

A short worked example captures the priority subtlety. Suppose the sampled state is `11:59:59 AM` with `reset=1` and `ena=1`. An enable-first model would carry seconds to `00`, minutes to `00`, hours to `12`, and toggle `pm` to PM, yielding `12:00:00 PM`. The correct result is `12:00:00 AM` with `pm=0`, because reset suppresses the entire increment, including the hour carry and the `pm` flip. This single row separates correct reset-priority logic from an enable-first model and also exercises the `pm` transition that a range-only checker would miss.

For the final Python checker, the most useful focus is an unconditional first-priority reset branch evaluated at every sampled posedge, combined with two independent invariant classes: BCD/range validity for all three fields, and explicit `pm` flip checks on exactly `11→12` and `12→01` with holds elsewhere. Range-only checking can miss a stuck or inverted `pm`; `pm`-only checking can miss malformed BCD such as `0x13` or `0x00`. Both are needed, and reset must dominate both.
