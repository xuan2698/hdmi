The public interface fixes the port widths tightly: `reset` and `ena` are each 1 bit, while `pm` is 1 bit and `hh`, `mm`, `ss` are each 8 bits. The 8-bit widths are exactly right for two BCD digits per field, but they also mean the checker must validate BCD legality rather than merely numeric range. For `hh`, legal values are 01 through 12; for `mm` and `ss`, 00 through 59. Each nibble must be 0–9, so values such as `0x1A` or `0x0F` are malformed even if they fall inside the 8-bit container. The checker should therefore test both nibble legality and field range, not just compare against a computed integer.

Boundary behavior is where the design is most exposed. Seconds and minutes wrap 59→00 and carry into the next field. Hours wrap 12→01, not 12→00 or 13→01, and that wrap toggles `pm`. The 11→12 transition also toggles `pm` without a carry from minutes or seconds. Thus `pm` changes on exactly two hour transitions: 11→12 and 12→01. A checker that only verifies numeric hour range can miss a stuck or inverted `pm`; a checker that only verifies `pm` can miss a malformed BCD hour. Both invariants are needed.

Reset is synchronous and has higher priority than enable. At a sampled posedge with `reset=1`, the expected next state is exactly `12:00:00 AM`, with `pm=0`, regardless of `ena` and regardless of the prior state. This must be the first branch in the checker's transition logic. If `reset=0` and `ena=0`, the entire state holds, including `pm`. If `reset=0` and `ena=1`, the increment proceeds normally. The dangerous coincidence is `reset=1, ena=1`: reset must suppress the increment entirely, including any carry into hours and any `pm` toggle.

A short worked example makes the priority issue concrete. Suppose the prior sampled state is `11:59:59 AM`, and the next posedge has `reset=1, ena=1`. The correct post-edge state is `12:00:00 AM`. An incorrect checker or model that applies enable first would carry seconds, minutes, and hours, reach `12:00:00`, and toggle `pm` to PM, yielding `12:00:00 PM`. That is wrong because reset wins outright. The checker should compare against the reset value before considering any increment.

The timing subtlety is that mid-cycle input changes occur after sampling and before the other edge, and are carried to the start of the next row. The checker should snapshot driven inputs at each posedge, evaluate the transition from that snapshot, compare outputs, and only then fold mid-cycle changes into the pending-input register for the next row. A post-edge reset must not retroactively alter the row already sampled.

Power-up is unspecified beyond reset semantics. The checker should treat state as unknown until the first sampled `reset=1` edge establishes `12:00:00 AM`, and should not compare outputs on earlier rows. The highest-value final-checker focus is an unconditional, first-priority reset branch at every posedge, paired with BCD/range invariants and explicit `pm` flip checks on 11→12 and 12→01.
