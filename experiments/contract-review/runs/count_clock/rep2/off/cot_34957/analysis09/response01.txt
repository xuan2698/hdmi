The key state is the four output registers plus `pm`, but the checker should treat the settled outputs as the observable state and derive expectations from the previous settled sample. The transition relation is gated first by `reset`, then by `ena`. Reset is synchronous and has priority: on a sampled edge with `reset=1`, the next settled outputs are exactly `pm=0`, `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, regardless of `ena` and regardless of the prior state. This must hold even when `ena=0`; a model that only applies reset while enabled is wrong. Because power-up is unspecified, the checker should not judge the first sampled edge unless it is a reset edge. A robust sequence starts with a reset sample, then checks from the following settled state onward.

When `reset=0` and `ena=0`, the settled outputs must be identical to the prior settled outputs. This includes `pm`, `hh`, `mm`, and `ss`. No field may advance, and no carry may occur. This row is important because it distinguishes an enable-gated counter from a free-running counter.

When `reset=0` and `ena=1`, the counter advances one second in BCD. Seconds `00` through `58` increment by one; `59` becomes `00` and carries into minutes. Minutes follow the same rule: `00` through `58` increment, `59` becomes `00` and carries into hours. Hours do not use ordinary BCD increment across the full range. The legal sequence is `12, 01, 02, ..., 11, 12`. Thus `12` goes to `01`, `01` goes to `02`, and so on through `11` going to `12`. The `pm` flag flips only on the `11→12` transition. It does not flip on `12→01`. Therefore the AM/PM boundary is tied to the hour rollover from 11 to 12, not to the wrap from 12 to 1.

A worked example: prior settled state is `11:59:59 AM`, so `pm=0`, `hh=0x11`, `mm=0x59`, `ss=0x59`. The sampled edge has `reset=0`, `ena=1`. Seconds roll `59→00`, minutes roll `59→00`, hours go `11→12`, and `pm` flips to 1. Expected settled outputs are `pm=1`, `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`. If inputs change after sampling but before the next edge, those changes affect only the next row, not this row’s expected result.

The checker should also reject illegal observed BCD values. `hh` must be in `0x01–0x12`; `mm` and `ss` must be in `0x00–0x59`. Illegal values are failures, not states to normalize. The most useful final-checker point is to include a reset-with-`ena=0` row from a non-reset prior state and require the exact reset outputs on that edge.
