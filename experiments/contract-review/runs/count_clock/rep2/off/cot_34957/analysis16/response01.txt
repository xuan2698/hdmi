The public interface is small but the boundary semantics are where a checker earns its keep. Inputs are one bit each: `reset` and `ena`. Outputs are `pm` (1 bit), `hh`, `mm`, `ss` (8 bits each). The 8-bit widths are exactly enough for two BCD digits, so every legal value has both nibbles in 0–9; anything else is malformed rather than merely out of range. `hh` is further constrained to 01–12, so `0x00` and `0x13`–`0x19` are invalid even though their nibbles are individually legal BCD. `mm` and `ss` are 00–59. `pm` is a state bit, not a function of `hh` alone: 12:xx exists in both AM and PM.

The timing contract matters more than the widths. `reset` is synchronous and active high, sampled on the rising edge, with priority over `ena`. The checker should latch each row's `reset` and `ena` at the sampled edge and compare the settled outputs after that edge against the transition implied by the previous settled state. Mid-row input changes happen after sampling and before the opposite edge; they belong to the next row and must never be used to justify the current row's outputs. The first row must be a reset row because power-up state is unspecified; without that anchor, a checker cannot distinguish a correct hold from an arbitrary starting point.

Two non-enabled rows are essential. A `reset=0, ena=0` hold row catches free-running counters and designs that increment on every `clk` instead of only on `ena`. A `reset=1, ena=0` row from a non-trivial state catches reset gated behind enable and catches reset to `00:00:00` instead of `12:00:00 AM`. After a reset row, the next row must either hold the reset state (if `reset=0, ena=0`) or advance from it (if `ena=1`).

Enabled increments require BCD carry and the 12-hour wrap. Seconds go 00→…→59→00 and carry into minutes; minutes carry into hours the same way. Hours go 01→…→11→12, then 12→01. The subtle point is `pm`: it toggles only on the 11→12 transition, not on 12→01. So `11:59:59 AM` with `ena=1` becomes `12:00:00 PM`, while `12:59:59 PM` with `ena=1` becomes `01:00:00 PM` with `pm` still 1. A checker that flips `pm` on every hour wrap, or on 12→01, will fail exactly these rows. Illegal BCD outputs should be rejected outright rather than normalized.

Worked example: settled state `pm=1, hh=0x11, mm=0x59, ss=0x59`; row inputs `reset=1, ena=0`. Expected next settled outputs are `pm=0, hh=0x12, mm=0x00, ss=0x00`. That one row checks reset priority over enable, the correct reset value rather than `00:00`, and suppression of any pending increment. The most useful point for the final checker: compare each row only against the previous settled state plus that row's latched inputs, and treat `pm` as part of the state transition rather than deriving it from `hh`.
