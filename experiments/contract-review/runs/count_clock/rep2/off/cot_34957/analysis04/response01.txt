The ports are all one-bit control signals except the three BCD output buses. `reset` and `ena` are each 1 bit; `pm` is 1 bit; `hh`, `mm`, and `ss` are each 8 bits, representing two BCD digits. For `hh`, the legal encoded values are `0x01` through `0x12` only, so the upper nibble is 0 or 1 and the lower nibble is constrained by the 12-hour range. For `mm` and `ss`, legal values are `0x00` through `0x59`, so the tens digit is 0–5 and the ones digit is 0–9. The checker should treat any other BCD encoding as outside the public specification rather than trying to assign meaning to it.

Boundary behavior is where most mistakes occur. Seconds and minutes roll over at `59` to `00`, carrying into the next higher field. Hours roll over at `12` to `01`, not to `00` and not to `13`. The AM/PM flag is not a simple function of the hour value alone; it toggles only on the `11→12` hour transition. Thus `11:59:59 AM` advances to `12:00:00 PM`, and `11:59:59 PM` advances to `12:00:00 AM`. By contrast, `12:59:59 PM` advances to `01:00:00 PM`, with `pm` unchanged. Reset is synchronous, active high, and has priority over `ena`; a sampled edge with `reset=1` must produce `pm=0`, `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00` regardless of enable or prior state.

A useful point for the final Python checker is to separate the sampled-edge decision from the settled-output comparison. At each sampled posedge, read the inputs as they are at that edge, compute the next state with reset first, then enable, then BCD counting, and only afterward compare the settled outputs. Mid-row changes must not retroactively alter the row already sampled. Also, because power-up state is unspecified, the checker should not assume initial register contents; it should establish a known state via a reset edge and begin checking from the following settled state.

Worked example: from `11:59:59 AM`, with `reset=0` and `ena=1` at the sampled edge, the next settled outputs are `pm=1`, `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`. If `ena` changes to `0` after that sampling but before the next edge, the next sampled edge holds `12:00:00 PM`; the earlier row remains as evaluated.
