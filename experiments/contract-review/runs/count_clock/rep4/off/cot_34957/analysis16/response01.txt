The interface is small but the widths encode BCD, not binary magnitude. `reset` and `ena` are each 1 bit; `pm` is 1 bit; `hh`, `mm`, and `ss` are each 8 bits, i.e. two BCD digits. The checker should treat each 8-bit field as two nibbles and validate that every observed value is a legal BCD pair, not merely within 0–255. Legal ranges are `hh` = 0x01–0x12, `mm` = 0x00–0x59, `ss` = 0x00–0x59. Any nibble above 9 is a protocol violation even if the byte is otherwise in range.

Boundary behavior is where most checker bugs hide. Seconds and minutes wrap at 0x59 to 0x00 and generate a carry. Hours wrap in the 12-hour sense: 0x12 → 0x01, not 0x13. The `pm` flag toggles only on the 11 → 12 transition, i.e. when `hh` was 0x11 and receives a carry. It does not toggle on 12 → 01; that wrap preserves `pm`. This asymmetry is the central subtlety: the AM/PM boundary is between 11 and 12, while the display wrap is between 12 and 01.

Reset is synchronous, active high, and has priority over `ena`. A sampled edge with `reset=1` must produce 12:00:00 AM (`hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=0`) regardless of `ena` and regardless of the prior displayed value. Reset can occur when `ena=0`; the counter still resets. The spec does not define power-on state, so the checker must not assume registers begin at 12:00 AM unless the testbench explicitly resets before the first sampled edge.

The dominant checker concern is sampling discipline. Inputs are driven before the sampled edge; optional mid-cycle changes occur after sampling and carry to the next row. Therefore latch `reset` and `ena` from the pre-edge snapshot, compute the expected next state from that snapshot plus the pre-edge displayed value, then compare against post-edge settled outputs. Reading `reset` or `ena` after the posedge risks missing a synchronous reset pulse or misattributing an enable. Also, `ena` is level-sensitive: if it is high in two consecutive pre-edge snapshots, the clock must increment twice. The checker must not require a rising edge on `ena`.

Worked example: pre-edge `hh=0x11`, `mm=0x59`, `ss=0x59`, `pm=0`, `reset=0`, `ena=1`. Expected next state: `ss` 0x59→0x00 with carry; `mm` 0x59→0x00 with carry; `hh` 0x11→0x12; `pm` toggles 0→1. Result 12:00:00 PM. If `reset=1` in that same snapshot, expected result is 12:00:00 AM regardless of `ena`.

For the final Python checker, focus on: latch inputs pre-edge; apply reset priority from the latched snapshot; treat `ena` as level-sensitive; implement BCD carries for `ss`, `mm`, and `hh`; and toggle `pm` only on the 11→12 hour carry. The main false-failure risks are post-edge input contamination and modeling `ena` as a one-shot pulse rather than a level.
