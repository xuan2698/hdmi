The public spec defines a synchronous 12-hour clock counter with BCD outputs and a PM flag. The checker should model the post-edge state as a pure function of the pre-edge snapshot, with `reset` taking absolute priority over `ena`. Because the spec says reset is synchronous and active high, the checker must sample `reset` and `ena` at the same edge as the state, then branch: if `reset` is high, force `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=0`; otherwise, if `ena` is high, advance the clock by one second; otherwise hold all outputs. The "hold" case is important: when `ena` is low and `reset` is low, no field may change, including `pm`. A checker that recomputes `pm` from the hour value on every row can still be correct, but it must not toggle `pm` merely because time passed without an enable.

The BCD structure is the main source of subtle errors. Seconds and minutes are two BCD digits each, so the legal values are `0x00` through `0x59`, and the carry from `ss` to `mm` occurs at `0x59` to `0x00`, not at a binary boundary. Likewise, `mm` carries to the hour at `0x59`. The hour field is legal only in `0x01` through `0x12`; values such as `0x00`, `0x13`, or any nibble above 9 are invalid. The checker should decode tens and units separately, verify each digit is 0-9, and verify the numeric range. This catches both malformed BCD and out-of-range hours.

The hour transition and `pm` behavior need explicit treatment. The hour sequence is `01,02,...,09,10,11,12,01,02,...`. The `pm` bit is not a free-running toggle; it changes only when the hour advances from 11 to 12. At that transition, `pm` flips. At the 12-to-1 wrap, `pm` does not flip. This means a binary modulo-12 model can appear correct for one transition and fail the next, especially if it treats 12 as zero or toggles on every wrap. The checker should express the hour carry in BCD terms: when minutes carry and the current hour is `0x11`, the next hour is `0x12` and `pm` toggles; when the current hour is `0x12`, the next hour is `0x01` and `pm` holds. For all other legal hours, increment the BCD value normally.

A useful worked example: pre-edge `hh=0x11`, `mm=0x59`, `ss=0x59`, `pm=0`, `ena=1`, `reset=0`. Post-edge must be `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=1`. If `reset=1` instead, post-edge must be `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=0`, and the enable-driven carry is ignored. The final checker should latch inputs before the sampled edge, branch on reset first, validate BCD digits and ranges, and implement the hour carry and PM toggle in BCD terms rather than binary modulo-12 arithmetic.
