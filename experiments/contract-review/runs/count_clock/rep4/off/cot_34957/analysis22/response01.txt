The public interface fixes widths tightly enough that the checker can reject malformed rows before any timing logic runs. `reset` and `ena` are each one bit, so only 0 and 1 are legal; anything else is a protocol violation. `pm` is one bit and must be exactly 0 or 1. `hh`, `mm`, and `ss` are each eight bits, but width alone is not a validity guarantee: each byte must be interpreted as two BCD nibbles. For `mm` and `ss`, both nibbles must be 0-9 and the combined value must lie in 0x00-0x59. For `hh`, both nibbles must be 0-9 and the combined value must lie in 0x01-0x12. Thus 0x00, 0x13, 0x1A, and 0xA0 are all invalid hours even though they fit in eight bits. The checker should validate these ranges on every sampled row, not only on rows where a carry is expected.

Boundary behavior is where a naive binary model diverges. Seconds and minutes wrap at 0x59 to 0x00, so the carry condition is "units digit is 9 and tens digit is 5," not "value equals 59 in binary arithmetic" applied loosely. The hour sequence is 01 through 09, then 10, 11, 12, then back to 01. The `pm` bit flips only on the 11-to-12 transition and holds on the 12-to-1 transition. A checker that computes `pm` as a function of hour alone, or that toggles on every hour wrap, will pass some rows and fail others. The safer approach is to latch the pre-edge inputs, branch on `reset` first, then on `ena`, and only then apply BCD increment rules.

A short worked example: pre-edge `hh=0x11`, `mm=0x59`, `ss=0x59`, `pm=0`, `ena=1`, `reset=0`. Post-edge must be `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=1`. If `reset=1` instead, post-edge must be `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=0`, and the enable-driven carry is ignored. The final checker should therefore validate BCD digits and ranges first, honor reset priority, preserve all outputs when `ena=0`, and implement the hour carry and PM toggle in BCD terms rather than binary modulo-12 arithmetic.
