One implementation mistake worth targeting in the final checker is treating the hour rollover as a simple modulo-12 increment on the BCD value, rather than as a BCD carry with a separate AM/PM toggle. The public spec defines `hh` as two BCD digits for hours 01–12 and `pm` as a separate indicator. That means the hour sequence is `01, 02, ..., 09, 10, 11, 12, 01, ...`, and the `pm` bit flips exactly when the hour advances from 11 to 12. A checker that computes hours by adding one to a binary interpretation and wrapping at 12 can accidentally accept or reject the wrong transition around `0x11`/`0x12`, especially if it also mishandles the BCD tens digit.

A useful worked example is the pre-edge snapshot `hh=0x11`, `mm=0x59`, `ss=0x59`, `pm=0`, `ena=1`, `reset=0`. Seconds roll from `0x59` to `0x00` with a minute carry; minutes roll from `0x59` to `0x00` with an hour carry; the hour advances from 11 to 12; and because the hour carry is 11→12, `pm` toggles from 0 to 1. The expected post-edge state is `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=1`. A naive modulo-12 binary model might produce `hh=0x12` here by coincidence, but it can fail on the next rollover: from `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x59`, `ss=0x59`, `pm=1`, `ena=1`, the expected state is `hh=0x01`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=0`. The same naive model may produce `0x00` or fail to toggle `pm` correctly.

The checker should therefore validate BCD digit structure as well as numeric range. Hours must be one of `0x01` through `0x12`; minutes and seconds must be `0x00` through `0x59` with valid BCD nibbles. The carry logic should be expressed in BCD terms: seconds increment with carry at `0x59`, minutes increment with carry at `0x59`, and hours increment through the 12-hour cycle with `pm` toggling only on the 11→12 transition. Reset must override all of this and force `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=0`.

Another related false-failure risk is applying reset after computing an enable-based increment. Since reset has higher priority than enable, the checker must branch on the latched pre-edge `reset` first. If `reset=1`, no carry or `pm` toggle from `ena` should be considered, even if `ena=1` in the same snapshot. Conversely, if `reset=0` and `ena=1`, the checker must perform exactly one increment from the pre-edge displayed state. Latching `reset` and `ena` before the sampled edge is essential; reading them after the edge can mix a mid-cycle change into the wrong row and produce spurious mismatches.
