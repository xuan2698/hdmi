The public interface is a synchronous 12-hour clock with BCD outputs and an AM/PM flag. Inputs are `clk`, `reset`, and `ena`, each one bit; outputs are `pm` (one bit), `hh` (8 bits), `mm` (8 bits), and `ss` (8 bits). The 8-bit widths for `hh`, `mm`, and `ss` are exactly enough for two BCD digits, since each digit uses four bits. The legal BCD encodings are therefore constrained: `ss` and `mm` range from `0x00` through `0x59`, with low nibbles 0–9 and high nibbles 0–5. `hh` ranges from `0x01` through `0x12`, with low nibbles 1–9 for 01–09 and 0–2 for 10–12, and high nibbles 0 or 1. Values such as `0x00`, `0x0A`, `0x60`, or `0x13` are outside the specified operating range and should not be treated as valid clock states.

`reset` is synchronous and active high. It has higher priority than `ena`, so at any sampled rising edge with `reset = 1`, the next state is 12:00:00 AM regardless of `ena`. The checker should evaluate reset before enable and must not let a simultaneous enable pulse advance the clock. This matters at rollover boundaries such as 11:59:59 AM to 12:00:00 PM and 11:59:59 PM to 12:00:00 AM: if reset and enable are both high at the sampled edge, the expected result is still 12:00:00 AM.

When reset is low and `ena` is high, seconds increment in BCD from 00 through 59. At 59, seconds wrap to 00 and minutes increment. Minutes likewise run 00 through 59; at 59 they wrap to 00 and hours increment. Hours follow the 12-hour sequence 01, 02, …, 11, 12, 01, … The AM/PM flag toggles only when hours roll from 11 to 12, not when hours roll from 12 to 01. Thus 11:59:59 AM advances to 12:00:00 PM, while 11:59:59 PM advances to 12:00:00 AM. When `ena` is low and reset is low, all outputs hold their previous values.

The mid-input timing rule is separate from the sampled-edge behavior. Optional mid-row input changes occur after sampling and before the other edge, and are carried to the start of the next row. A reset asserted mid-row must not alter outputs already sampled for that row; it becomes the pending reset value for the next posedge. The checker should maintain a pending-input register distinct from the sampled row and compare outputs only at posedge boundaries.

Worked example: if the sampled row is 11:59:59 AM with `ena = 1` and `reset = 0`, the expected next state is 12:00:00 PM with `pm = 1` and `hh = 0x12`. If reset is asserted mid-row afterward, that change does not modify the already-sampled 12:00:00 PM row; it is carried to the next boundary, where reset priority yields 12:00:00 AM.

For the final Python checker, the key point is to model reset as a synchronous highest-priority input evaluated at each sampled posedge, with a separate pending-input path for mid-row changes. Do not infer an undefined power-up state, and do not compare outputs immediately after mid-input changes.
