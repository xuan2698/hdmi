The key distinguishing case for the checker is the 11→12 hour boundary combined with a full minute/second rollover, because it simultaneously exercises BCD carry across all three fields and the only `pm` toggle in the design. A correct implementation must produce 12:00:00 with `pm` flipped, while a buggy one might either fail to carry the minutes/seconds or flip `pm` on the wrong hour edge.

Worked example: suppose the settled state is 11:59:59 AM (`hh=8'h11`, `mm=8'h59`, `ss=8'h59`, `pm=0`). With `ena=1` and `reset=0` sampled at the next posedge, seconds roll 59→00, minutes roll 59→00, hours roll 11→12, and `pm` toggles to 1, giving 12:00:00 PM. If instead `reset=1` is sampled at that edge, the result is 12:00:00 AM (`pm=0`) regardless of `ena`, since reset has priority.

For the final Python checker, the useful point is to model each sampled posedge as one atomic transition from the previous settled state, applying reset first and then enable, and to update `pm` only on the 11→12 hour transition—never on 12→01, and never on minute or second rollovers. The checker should also avoid inventing a power-on state, since the spec defines only the synchronous reset state, and should compare only settled outputs at edge boundaries, carrying mid-row input changes forward to the next sampled edge rather than applying them retroactively.
