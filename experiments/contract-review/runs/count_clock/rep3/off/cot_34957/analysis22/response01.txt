The public interface fixes the port widths unambiguously: `reset` and `ena` are each 1 bit; `pm` is 1 bit; and `hh`, `mm`, and `ss` are each 8 bits, i.e. two BCD digits per field. The 8-bit widths are wide enough to hold any legal two-digit BCD value (00–99), but the legal ranges are narrower: `hh` is 01–12, `mm` is 00–59, and `ss` is 00–59. The checker should therefore not infer legality from width alone. A value such as `hh = 8'h00`, `hh = 8'h13`, or any field containing a non-BCD nibble (A–F) is outside the specified behavior and should be treated as a mismatch rather than normalized or wrapped.

Boundary values matter most at the carry points. For seconds, the critical pair is 58→59 and 59→00; the latter is the only seconds transition that can affect minutes. For minutes, the critical pair is 58→59 and 59→00; the latter is the only minutes transition that can affect hours. For hours, the sequence is 01 through 12 and then back to 01, so the two distinct hour boundaries are 11→12 and 12→01. These are not symmetric with respect to `pm`. The 11→12 transition is the only place `pm` changes: 11:xx:xx AM becomes 12:xx:xx PM, and 11:xx:xx PM becomes 12:xx:xx AM. The 12→01 transition leaves `pm` unchanged. Minute and second rollovers never change `pm`. This asymmetry is the main place a checker can go wrong: an hour counter that cycles 01–12 correctly can still have incorrect `pm` if it toggles on 12→01 or fails to toggle on 11→12.

Reset is synchronous, active high, and has priority over enable. It can be asserted whether or not `ena` is high. When reset is sampled high at a posedge, the settled outputs become `hh = 8'h12`, `mm = 8'h00`, `ss = 8'h00`, and `pm = 0`, regardless of the previous state and regardless of `ena`. When reset is low and `ena` is high, the clock advances by one second using the rollover order seconds → minutes → hours with BCD carry. When reset is low and `ena` is low, the settled outputs hold their previous values.

The checker should treat each sampled posedge as one atomic transition from the previous settled state, using the pending input values driven before that edge. Mid-row input changes occur after sampling and are carried to the next sampled edge; they do not retroactively alter the already-sampled outputs. The specification defines a reset state but does not define an independent asynchronous power-on state, so the checker should not invent power-up behavior. It should compare only settled outputs at edge boundaries, apply reset before enable, and update `pm` only on the 11→12 hour transition.

Worked example: from 11:59:59 AM with `ena = 1` and `reset = 0`, the next sampled edge yields 12:00:00 PM (`pm = 1`). With `reset = 1` at that same edge, the result is 12:00:00 AM (`pm = 0`), because reset dominates enable.
