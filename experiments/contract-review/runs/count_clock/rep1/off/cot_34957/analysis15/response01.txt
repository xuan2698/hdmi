The public spec defines a 12-hour clock counter with BCD outputs and an AM/PM flag. The key structural point for the checker is that the design has a single synchronous state vector consisting of `hh`, `mm`, `ss`, and `pm`, and every sampled rising edge of `clk` produces a new state according to a priority-ordered rule set. The checker should model this as a transition relation over sampled edges, not as a continuous-time process.

The priority order is: reset first, then enable, then hold. When `reset=1` on a sampled edge, the next state is unconditionally `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=0`. This override applies regardless of the prior state, regardless of `ena`, and regardless of whether the prior state was legal. The checker must not require any precondition on the pre-edge state for a reset edge to be accepted. In particular, a reset pulse may interrupt an otherwise enabled counting sequence, and the checker must accept the jump to `12:00 AM` without attempting to reconcile it with a one-second increment. The worked example in the spec illustrates this: from `11:59:59 AM` with `ena=1` and `reset=1`, the next state is `12:00:00 AM`, not `12:00:00 PM`; the `11→12` PM toggle must not be applied on a reset edge.

When `reset=0` and `ena=0`, the state must hold exactly: `hh`, `mm`, `ss`, and `pm` all retain their pre-edge values. This hold rule applies to `pm` as well, so the checker must verify `pm` stability on held edges, not just the BCD digits.

When `reset=0` and `ena=1`, the counter increments by one second in BCD, with the standard cascading carries: seconds `00–59`, minutes `00–59`, hours `01–12`. The `pm` flag toggles only at the two legal 12-hour rollover points: `11→12` and `12→01`. All other hour increments preserve `pm`. The checker should verify that `pm` changes exactly at those two transitions and nowhere else on enabled non-reset edges.

Power-up is unspecified. The checker should not assume any initial state, should not inject an implicit reset, and should not require that the first enabled edge behaves as if starting from `12:00 AM`. It should begin transition checking only after observing a sampled `reset=1` edge, or restrict itself to invariant checks such as BCD legality (each digit in `0–9`, hours in `01–12`, minutes and seconds in `00–59`) and the `pm`/hour consistency rule. BCD legality should be enforced as an invariant on every sampled edge, including reset edges, rather than as an initialization assumption.

The final Python checker should therefore: (1) treat reset as a synchronous override valid from any prior state; (2) treat power-up as unknown until reset is observed; (3) enforce BCD legality as an invariant; and (4) apply the hold and enable/rollover rules, including the `pm` toggle points, only on non-reset sampled edges.
