The public interface is small but the boundary semantics are where a checker is most likely to go wrong. Inputs are one bit each: `reset` and `ena`. Outputs are `pm` (one bit), and `hh`, `mm`, `ss` (eight bits each). The eight-bit outputs are not binary integers; they are two packed BCD digits, one per nibble. So `hh=0x12` means “12”, not decimal 18. The checker must decode or compare nibble-wise, not by ordinary integer arithmetic.

The legal value ranges are asymmetric. `ss` and `mm` are 00–59: each nibble’s low digit is 0–9 and high digit is 0–5. `hh` is 01–12: it is never `0x00`; the tens nibble is 0 or 1; if tens is 0 the ones nibble is 1–9, and if tens is 1 the ones nibble is 0–2. `pm` is independent and simply records whether the displayed 12-hour time is AM or PM. Reset forces `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=0`, i.e. 12:00:00 AM. Reset is synchronous and has priority over `ena`; it may be sampled with `ena` low or high. A checker must therefore not assume the first observed row is 12:00 AM unless a reset was actually sampled at that edge. The initial state before any sampled reset is unspecified by the public spec.

When `reset=0` and `ena=1`, exactly one second is added. The carry chain is BCD: `ss` increments unless it is `0x59`, in which case it becomes `0x00` and carries to `mm`; `mm` behaves identically and carries to `hh`. The hour rule is the subtle part. The 12-hour sequence is 01,02,…,11,12,01,02,… and `pm` changes at both boundaries: 11→12 is AM→PM, and 12→1 is PM→AM. Thus a carry into hours from `hh=0x11` produces `hh=0x12` and toggles `pm`; a carry from `hh=0x12` produces `hh=0x01` and toggles `pm`; any other carried hour increments normally without changing `pm`. When `reset=0` and `ena=0`, all outputs hold.

A useful worked boundary for the final checker: previous state 11:59:59 AM (`hh=0x11`, `mm=0x59`, `ss=0x59`, `pm=0`) with `reset=0`, `ena=1` must yield 12:00:00 PM (`hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=1`). The same previous state with `reset=1` must yield 12:00:00 AM (`pm=0`) regardless of `ena`. The checker should explicitly test both hour-boundary toggles, the no-toggle ordinary hour increment, the `0x59` minute/second wraps, the hold case, and reset priority. It should also reject any enabled transition that leaves `hh=0x00`, a non-BCD nibble, or an out-of-range minute/second. Because `ena` is a pulse, enabled rows need not be evenly spaced; only one increment per enabled sampled edge is required.
