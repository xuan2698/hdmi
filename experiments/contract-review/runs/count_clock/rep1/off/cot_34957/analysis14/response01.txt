The reset and power-up behavior is the part most likely to be mishandled by a naive checker, so it deserves careful separation from the normal counting rules.

Reset is synchronous and active high. That means the checker must only demand the reset state after a sampled edge where `reset=1` was present. It must not assume that the design is in `12:00 AM` merely because simulation has just started, because power-up state is not specified by the public interface. If the first sampled edge has `reset=0`, the checker has no license to require any particular `hh`, `mm`, `ss`, or `pm` value. The only safe assumptions before the first reset are the structural BCD legality constraints that any legal clock state must satisfy: `ss` and `mm` must be valid BCD in the ranges `00`–`59`, and `hh` must be one of `01`–`12`. Even those should be treated as output-legality checks, not as an initialization contract.

Reset has higher priority than enable. Therefore, on any sampled edge where `reset=1`, the next state must be exactly `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, and `pm=0`, regardless of the sampled `ena` value and regardless of the prior state. The checker should not require that the previous state was legal, nor that `ena` was asserted, nor that any particular number of cycles has elapsed. A reset edge is a complete override. This also means that a reset pulse can appear in the middle of an otherwise enabled sequence, and the checker must accept the jump to `12:00 AM` without trying to reconcile it with a one-second increment.

Power-up behavior is deliberately unspecified. The public spec gives no initial values, no reset-at-time-zero guarantee, and no requirement that the first enabled edge behaves as if starting from `12:00 AM`. A correct checker should therefore begin checking only after it has observed a sampled `reset=1` edge, or it should restrict itself to invariant checks such as BCD legality and the `pm`/hour consistency rule. It should not inject an implicit reset at the start of simulation unless the testbench explicitly drives one.

The `pm` indicator is part of the state and must be checked on every sampled edge, including reset edges. On reset, `pm` must become `0`. On a held edge with `ena=0` and `reset=0`, `pm` must hold exactly along with `hh`, `mm`, and `ss`. On an enabled non-reset edge, `pm` changes only according to the 12-hour rollover rules, not on every hour carry. The two legal toggle points are `11→12` and `12→01`; all other hour increments preserve `pm`.

A short worked example clarifies the reset priority. Suppose the pre-edge sampled state is `hh=0x11`, `mm=0x59`, `ss=0x59`, `pm=0`, with `ena=1` and `reset=1`. The next state must be `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=0`, not the enabled increment to `12:00:00 PM`. The reset wins, and the checker must not apply the `11→12` `pm` toggle in this case.

For the final Python checker, the useful rule is: treat reset as a synchronous override that is valid from any prior state, treat power-up as unknown until reset is observed, and enforce BCD legality as an invariant rather than as an initialization assumption. Then apply the enable/hold and `pm` rollover rules only on non-reset sampled edges.
