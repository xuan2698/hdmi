The clock is a synchronous BCD timekeeper with three cascaded counters and an AM/PM flag. On each sampled rising edge, `reset` dominates: if asserted, the next state is unconditionally `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=0`, regardless of `ena`. Otherwise, if `ena=0`, all four outputs hold their previous values. Only when `reset=0` and `ena=1` does the counter advance.

The seconds counter increments by one BCD count on each enabled edge. From `ss=0x00` through `0x58` it simply adds one. At `ss=0x59` it wraps to `0x00` and emits a carry into minutes. Minutes behave identically: `0x00`–`0x58` increment, `0x59` wraps to `0x00` and carries into hours. Hours are the subtle part. The legal hour codes are `0x01` through `0x12` inclusive; `0x00` and `0x13` are never valid. An ordinary hour increment goes `0x01→0x02`, …, `0x11→0x12`. The special transitions are `0x12→0x01` with a toggle of `pm`, and `0x11→0x12` with no toggle. Thus the AM/PM flag flips exactly when the hour wraps from 12 to 1, not when it reaches 12. The hour carry from minutes therefore has two distinct outcomes depending on the current hour: if `hh=0x11`, the next hour is `0x12` and `pm` is unchanged; if `hh=0x12`, the next hour is `0x01` and `pm` toggles. No other hour value can receive a minute carry, because a carry only occurs at `mm=0x59` and the hour must then be either 11 or 12 for the state to be reachable.

A useful checker point is the asymmetry at the 11→12→1 boundary. Consider the row `hh=0x11`, `mm=0x59`, `ss=0x59`, `pm=0`, `ena=1`, `reset=0`. The next sampled state must be `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=0`. The following enabled edge from that state must be `hh=0x01`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=1`. A checker that increments hours as an integer and toggles `pm` on every hour carry, or that toggles only on the 12→1 transition but forgets the 11→12 no-toggle, will fail one of these two rows. The checker should compare each enabled sampled edge only to the immediately preceding sampled state, since `ena` need not be periodic; disabled rows are pure holds and must not be treated as increments. It should also reject any next state with a BCD nibble above 9, `hh` outside `0x01`–`0x12`, or `mm`/`ss` outside `0x00`–`0x59`, and it must not assume the first sampled row is 12:00 AM unless a reset was actually sampled at that edge.
