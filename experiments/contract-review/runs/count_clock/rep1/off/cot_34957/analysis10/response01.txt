The interface is small but the state space is not: four outputs (`pm`, `hh`, `mm`, `ss`) plus two inputs (`reset`, `ena`), all sampled on `clk`. Widths are fixed: `reset` and `ena` are 1 bit; `pm` is 1 bit; `hh`, `mm`, and `ss` are 8 bits each, interpreted as two BCD digits. That means each of `hh`, `mm`, `ss` has only 100 possible bit patterns, but the legal subset is much smaller: `hh` ∈ {0x01..0x12} (12 values), `mm` ∈ {0x00..0x59} (60 values), `ss` ∈ {0x00..0x59} (60 values). The full legal state count is therefore 12 × 60 × 60 × 2 = 86,400, exactly the number of seconds in 12 hours. Any sampled output outside those sets is a specification violation regardless of inputs.

Boundary values matter more than the interior. For `ss` and `mm`, the critical codes are `0x00`, `0x09`, `0x10`, `0x58`, and `0x59`. The increment `0x09→0x10` is a BCD carry within the byte, not a binary add; `0x59→0x00` is the wrap that emits a carry. For `hh`, the critical codes are `0x01`, `0x09`, `0x10`, `0x11`, and `0x12`. The transitions `0x09→0x10` and `0x11→0x12` are ordinary BCD increments; `0x12→0x01` is the wrap that toggles `pm`. There is no legal `0x00` hour and no legal `0x13` hour, so a checker must not accept either as a next state even transiently.

Priority is explicit: `reset` is synchronous, active high, and dominates `ena`. A sampled edge with `reset=1` forces `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=0` (12:00 AM), regardless of `ena`. A sampled edge with `reset=0` and `ena=0` forces all four outputs to hold their previous sampled values. Only `reset=0, ena=1` advances the counter. Because `ena` need not be periodic, the checker must treat each enabled edge as one increment from the immediately preceding sampled state, not from an assumed cadence.

The subtle point for the final checker is the hour-carry asymmetry. A minute carry occurs only when `mm=0x59` and `ss=0x59` on an enabled edge. At that moment the hour must be either `0x11` or `0x12` for a reachable state. If `hh=0x11`, the next hour is `0x12` and `pm` does not change. If `hh=0x12`, the next hour is `0x01` and `pm` toggles. A checker that toggles `pm` on every hour carry, or that treats `0x12→0x01` as the only special case while mishandling `0x11→0x12`, will fail. Worked example: from `hh=0x11, mm=0x59, ss=0x59, pm=0, ena=1, reset=0`, the next sampled state must be `hh=0x12, mm=0x00, ss=0x00, pm=0`; the following enabled edge must be `hh=0x01, mm=0x00, ss=0x00, pm=1`.

Finally, the checker should not assume the first sampled row is 12:00 AM unless a reset was actually sampled at that edge. The initial state is whatever the first sampled outputs are, provided they are legal; reset is the only mechanism that guarantees 12:00 AM.
