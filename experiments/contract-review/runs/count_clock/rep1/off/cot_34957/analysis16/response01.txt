The public interface is a synchronous 12-hour clock counter with a fast `clk`, a one-bit enable `ena`, a one-bit active-high synchronous reset `reset`, and outputs `pm` (1 bit), `hh` (8 bits), `mm` (8 bits), and `ss` (8 bits). The 8-bit hour, minute, and second outputs are each two BCD digits, so the legal encodings are constrained: each nibble must be 0–9, hours must represent 01–12, minutes 00–59, and seconds 00–59. The `pm` output is a single flag: 1 for PM, 0 for AM. Reset is synchronous and has priority over enable; it forces the displayed time to 12:00 AM, i.e. `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=0`.

The key boundary values are the BCD rollover points. Seconds and minutes count 00 through 59, then wrap to 00 with a carry. Hours count 01 through 12, then wrap to 01. The AM/PM flag toggles only at the two 12-hour boundaries: 11→12 and 12→01. Thus, on an enabled non-reset edge, `pm` must change exactly when the hour transition is 11→12 or 12→01, and must remain unchanged for every other hour increment, including 01→02 through 10→11 and the minute/second-only increments. The reset target is always 12:00 AM, so a reset edge from any prior state—including 11:59:59 AM or 11:59:59 PM—must land on `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=0`, without applying any ordinary increment or PM toggle.

The checker should treat reset as a synchronous override valid from any pre-edge state. It must not require any precondition on the pre-edge state for a reset edge to be accepted, and it must not try to reconcile a reset jump with a one-second increment. For example, from `11:59:59 AM` with `ena=1` and `reset=1`, the next state is `12:00:00 AM`, not `12:00:00 PM`; the 11→12 PM toggle must not be applied on a reset edge. When `reset=0` and `ena=0`, all outputs—`hh`, `mm`, `ss`, and `pm`—must hold exactly. The checker must verify `pm` stability on held edges as well as the BCD digits. When `reset=0` and `ena=1`, the counter increments by one second with standard cascading carries, and the `pm` toggle rule applies only at the two legal rollover points.

Power-up is unspecified. The checker should not assume an initial state, should not inject an implicit reset, and should not require the first enabled edge to behave as if starting from 12:00 AM. Transition checking should begin only after a sampled `reset=1` edge has been observed, or else be limited to invariant checks such as BCD legality and the `pm`/hour consistency rule. BCD legality should be enforced on every sampled edge, including reset edges, rather than treated as an initialization assumption. The final Python checker should therefore: treat reset as a synchronous override from any prior state; treat power-up as unknown until reset is observed; enforce BCD legality as an invariant; and apply hold and enable/rollover rules, including the `pm` toggle points, only on non-reset sampled edges.
