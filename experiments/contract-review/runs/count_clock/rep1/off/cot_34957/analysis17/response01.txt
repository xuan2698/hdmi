The key distinguishing case for the checker is a reset edge that coincides with an enabled rollover boundary, because that is where a naive implementation is most likely to leak an ordinary increment or PM toggle into the reset result. Consider a pre-edge state of `11:59:59 AM` (`hh=0x11`, `mm=0x59`, `ss=0x59`, `pm=0`) with `ena=1` and `reset=1`. A correct synchronous-reset design ignores `ena` entirely on this edge and loads the reset target: `hh=0x12`, `mm=0x00`, `ss=0x00`, `pm=0`. A buggy design that computes the increment first and then applies reset, or that applies the 11→12 PM toggle before reset, could instead produce `12:00:00 PM` or some hybrid. The checker must therefore assert the reset target directly from the sampled pre-edge state, with no requirement that the pre-edge state be "compatible" with a one-second step, and with no PM toggle applied on that edge.

This example also exposes the power-up subtlety. Before any sampled `reset=1` edge, the checker cannot know whether the DUT is at `12:00 AM`, at some arbitrary BCD-legal time, or at an illegal value. So it must not inject an implicit reset, must not assume the first enabled edge starts from `12:00 AM`, and must not flag a "wrong" first transition. Instead, before reset is observed, it should limit itself to invariants that hold regardless of history: BCD legality of `hh`, `mm`, and `ss` on every sampled edge (including reset edges), and the `pm`/hour consistency rule (for example, `pm` must be consistent with the 12-hour encoding the design uses, and `hh` must lie in the legal 01–12 range). Only after a sampled `reset=1` edge establishes a known state should the checker begin full transition checking.

Once reset has been observed, the checker should apply three rules on each subsequent sampled edge. First, if `reset=1`, the next state is unconditionally `12:00:00 AM` with `pm=0`, regardless of `ena` and regardless of the pre-edge state; no increment and no PM toggle are applied. Second, if `reset=0` and `ena=0`, every output must hold exactly, including `pm`; the checker must verify PM stability on held edges, not just the BCD digits. Third, if `reset=0` and `ena=1`, the counter increments by one second with standard cascading carries, and `pm` toggles only on the 11→12 and 12→01 hour transitions; it must remain unchanged for 01→02 through 10→11 and for minute/second-only increments.

A useful worked check: from `11:59:59 AM` with `ena=1`, `reset=0`, the next state is `12:00:00 PM` (`pm` toggles on 11→12). From `12:59:59 PM` with `ena=1`, `reset=0`, the next state is `01:00:00 PM` (`pm` toggles on 12→01). From `10:59:59 AM` with `ena=1`, `reset=0`, the next state is `11:00:00 AM` (`pm` unchanged). The checker should encode these as explicit expected transitions after reset is known, and treat the reset-coincident rollover as a separate override case rather than as a special increment.
