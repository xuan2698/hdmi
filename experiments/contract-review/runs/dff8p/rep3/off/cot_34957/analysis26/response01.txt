The key hazard for the checker is that the specification gives the reset value, not the power-up value. A checker that initializes its reference model to `0x34` and begins comparing immediately can fail on the first rows even though the design is correct. The safe approach is to defer assertions on `q` until after the first sampled negative edge with pending `reset` high, or until the test has otherwise established a known committed value. Until then, the checker should track pending inputs and the sampled edge but not require any particular `q`.

Another subtle mistake is to confuse a data load of `0x00` with a reset. Since reset is active high and synchronous, `d = 0x00` with `reset = 0` must load zero into `q` at the sampled negative edge. A checker that uses zero as a sentinel for “no update” or that treats zero data as a reset will incorrectly expect `q` to become `0x34`. The reference model should represent pending `d` and pending `reset` separately, and it should commit `q` from the edge evaluation without special-casing zero data.

A short worked example: suppose committed `q` is `0x34`. After a sampled edge, the test drives `d = 0x00` and keeps `reset = 0`. Committed `q` remains `0x34` until the next sampled negative edge. At that edge, pending `reset` is low, so `q` commits to pending `d`, namely `0x00`. If the checker treats zero as “no load” or as a reset indicator, it will wrongly expect `0x34` and report a failure. The correct model commits `0x00`.

For the final Python checker, the useful focus is to maintain pending `d` and pending `reset`, apply mid-cycle changes only to those pending values, evaluate next state solely at the sampled negative edge, and commit `q` from that evaluation. Assertions on `q` should begin only after the first reset-sampled edge or another known committed state. This avoids asynchronous-reset modeling, reset-to-zero assumptions, premature sampling, and confusion between a legitimate zero data load and a reset event. The checker should treat `0x34` as the value committed by a reset-sampled edge, not as a universal initial condition.
