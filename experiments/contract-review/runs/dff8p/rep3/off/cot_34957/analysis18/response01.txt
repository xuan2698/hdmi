One common implementation mistake is to treat the active-high synchronous reset as if it were asynchronous, or to let mid-cycle input changes affect the committed state before the next sampled negative edge. The public specification says the eight D flip-flops are triggered by the negative edge of `clk`, and reset is synchronous and active high. That means the reset value `0x34` is committed only when a sampled negative edge occurs while `reset` is high. A mid-cycle assertion of `reset` must not immediately force `q` to `0x34`; it is merely a pending input for the next sampled edge. Likewise, a mid-cycle change on `d` must not alter `q` until the next sampled negative edge. The checker should therefore separate pending inputs from committed state and evaluate the next state only at the sampled edge.

A related mistake is to assume the flip-flops power up at zero or at `0x34`. The public contract does not define power-up state. It only defines behavior at sampled negative edges. The checker should avoid asserting `q` before the first reset-sampled edge, and it should not infer an initial value from the reset constant. The reset constant `0x34` is the value loaded on a reset-sampled edge, not necessarily the initial state.

Another useful point for the final checker is that there is no enable, load, or asynchronous clear. At a sampled negative edge, the committed next state is exactly `0x34` if `reset` is high at that edge, otherwise the pending `d` value. No other condition should modify `q`. If `reset` and `d` both change mid-cycle, both are pending until the next sampled negative edge. If `reset` is high at that edge, it dominates and `q` becomes `0x34` regardless of `d`.

A short worked example: suppose committed `q` is `0x34`. After a sampled edge, `d` changes to `0x7A` while `reset` stays low. Committed `q` remains `0x34`; the new `d` is pending. At the next sampled negative edge, if no newer `d` has been driven, `q` becomes `0x7A`. If instead the next row drives `d = 0x11` before that edge, `q` becomes `0x11`. If `reset` is high at that edge, `q` becomes `0x34` regardless of `d`.

For the final Python checker, the robust approach is to maintain pending `d` and pending `reset`, update them on row drives and mid-cycle changes, evaluate only at the sampled negative edge, commit `q` from that evaluation, and defer `q` assertions until after the first reset-sampled edge. This avoids the common failures of asynchronous reset modeling, reset-to-zero assumptions, and premature or delayed sampling.
