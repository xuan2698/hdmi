The specification describes eight D flip-flops sharing a single clock, a single active-high synchronous reset, and a common 8-bit data input. The output is the 8-bit committed state `q`. The reset value is deliberately nonzero (`0x34`), and that is the main source of modeling mistakes.

The most important distinction is between pending inputs and committed state. A change on `d` or `reset` between sampled edges does not affect `q` immediately. It only updates the pending value that will be examined at the next negative edge. If both inputs change mid-cycle, both changes remain pending. At the next sampled edge, reset has priority: a high `reset` forces `q` to `0x34` even if `d` is also pending. If `reset` is low at that edge, the pending `d` is committed. No other condition should modify `q`.

Power-up behavior is not defined by the public contract. The specification does not say whether the flip-flops initialize to zero, to `0x34`, or to any other value. The constant `0x34` is the value loaded on a reset-sampled edge, not necessarily the initial state. A checker should therefore avoid asserting `q` before the first sampled negative edge at which reset is high, or more generally before the first sampled edge that establishes a known committed value under the test's assumptions. Inferring an initial value from the reset constant is unsafe.

A short worked example: suppose committed `q` is `0x34`. After a sampled edge, `d` changes to `0x7A` while `reset` remains low. Committed `q` stays `0x34`; `0x7A` is merely pending. At the next sampled negative edge, if no newer `d` has been driven, `q` becomes `0x7A`. If a later row drives `d = 0x11` before that edge, `q` becomes `0x11`. If `reset` is high at that edge, `q` becomes `0x34` regardless of `d`.

For the final Python checker, the useful focus is to separate pending inputs from committed output and to sample only at the negative edge. Maintain pending `d` and pending `reset`, update them on row drives and mid-cycle changes, evaluate the next state only at the sampled negative edge, commit `q` from that evaluation, and defer `q` assertions until after the first reset-sampled edge. This avoids the common failures of asynchronous reset modeling, reset-to-zero assumptions, and premature or delayed sampling.
