The key subtlety for the checker is that the flag is a *pre-edge* qualifier: the decision to increment on a low input depends on whether the flag was already set before that rising edge, not on whether the current edge sets it. This creates a one-cycle "arming" delay after any high input, and that delay must be modeled explicitly rather than folded into the output.

A useful distinguishing example is the transition from a long high run into a low run. Suppose the state is `q = 4`, `flag = 0`, and `a` has been 1 for several edges. At the first rising edge where `a = 0`, the old flag is still 0, so `q` stays 4 and the flag becomes 1. Only at the *next* rising edge with `a = 0` does `q` advance to 5. If a checker instead incremented immediately on the first low edge, it would produce `q = 5` one cycle too early and then remain offset for the rest of the low run. Likewise, if a checker cleared the flag on a low edge or failed to preserve it on incrementing low edges, it would either stall or increment on every low edge, both of which contradict the trace.

The trace also shows modulo-8 wrap: at 75 ns the rising edge takes `q` from 6 to 0, not to 7. So the increment must be `(q + 1) % 8`, and the checker should compare the wrapped value directly rather than assuming a wider counter.

Finally, the initial unknown at time 0 should not be treated as a mismatch. The first meaningful comparison is the 5 ns rising edge, where the trace gives `q = 4`. Initializing `q = 4` and `flag = 0` before that edge reproduces the observed hold at 5 ns through 45 ns under `a = 1`, then the arming hold at 45 ns, then increments at 55, 65, 75, and 85 ns. The checker should therefore seed both state variables, sample after the posedge settles, and update `q` and `flag` together from the old values.
