Looking at the waveform, the key observation is that `q` only changes on rising edges of `clk`, and only when `a = 0`. When `a = 1`, `q` holds at 4 indefinitely. When `a = 0`, `q` increments by 1 on each rising edge, wrapping from 6 to 0 (and presumably 7 to 0, though 7 never appears here).

Let me trace the edge timing carefully. The sampled rows are taken after the posedge has settled, with inputs driven before the edge. So at 5ns (posedge), `a=1`, `q` becomes 4 — this is the initial load. At 15ns, 25ns, 35ns, 45ns: all `a=1`, `q` stays 4. At 55ns: `a=0`, `q` goes 4→5. At 65ns: `a=0`, `q` goes 5→6. At 75ns: `a=0`, `q` goes 6→0 (wrap). At 85ns: `a=0`, `q` goes 0→1.

So the behavior is: on each rising edge, if `a=1`, load 4; if `a=0`, increment `q` modulo 8 (3-bit wrap). This is a loadable up-counter with load value 4 and enable = ~a.

The mid-input changes matter for the checker: the spec says optional mid changes occur after sampling, before the other edge, carried to the next row. In this trace, `a` changes at 45ns (from 1 to 0) — but note the row at 45ns already shows `a=0` sampled at the posedge. Actually the transition of `a` from 1 to 0 happens between 40ns and 45ns, so at the 45ns posedge `a=0` is already stable. The checker must ensure inputs are stable at the posedge (setup), and that any mid-cycle change is applied after the previous sample and held through the next posedge.

Worked example: at 65ns posedge, `a=0` (stable since before the edge), `q` was 5, so `q` becomes 6. At 75ns posedge, `a=0`, `q=6`, so `q` wraps to 0. The wrap is modulo 8 because `q` is 3 bits.

Verify each sampled row matches this recurrence, and verify that `a` is stable across each posedge (no mid-edge glitch).
