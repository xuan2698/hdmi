The circuit is a 3-bit modulo-7 counter with a synchronous load: on each rising edge, if the sampled `a` is 1, `q` loads 4; if `a` is 0, `q` increments as `(q + 1) mod 7`. The initial `q` is `x`, so the checker should allow an undefined value until the first rising edge. The subtlety is the sampling convention: each row's printed `a` is driven before the sampled edge, but optional mid-interval changes occur after sampling and carry into the next row. Thus the value sampled at a given posedge may differ from that row's printed `a`.

Worked example: at 40 ns, `q = 4` and the carried input is 1. The 45 ns posedge samples that carried 1, so `q` loads 4; after sampling, `a` becomes 0. At 55 ns the posedge samples 0, so `q` becomes `(4 + 1) mod 7 = 5`. A same-row checker would wrongly increment at 45 ns. The final checker must track the pre-edge input separately from the row's printed `a`.
