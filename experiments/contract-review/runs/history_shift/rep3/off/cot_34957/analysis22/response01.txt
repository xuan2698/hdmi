The port widths are fixed and narrow: all control inputs are 1 bit, `train_history` and `predict_history` are 32 bits, and `clk` is the only clock. The checker should therefore treat every control signal as a boolean and every history value as an unsigned 32-bit word, with no intermediate widths beyond 32 bits. The key boundary is the LSB: `predict_taken` enters at bit 0, and `train_taken` replaces bit 0 when a misprediction loads the register. The MSB is discarded on every shift, so `32'h8000_0000` shifted left becomes `32'h0000_0000`, not a 33-bit value.

The decisive boundary case is simultaneous assertion of `predict_valid` and `train_mispredicted`. The spec gives misprediction priority because the flush removes the predicting branch. Thus the next state must be `{train_history[30:0], train_taken}`, independent of `predict_taken`. A checker that shifts first, or that concatenates from the current register rather than `train_history`, will fail this row.

Worked example: after reset, `predict_valid=1, predict_taken=1` yields `32'h0000_0001`. Next edge, assert `train_mispredicted=1, train_taken=0, train_history=32'h0000_0001` together with `predict_valid=1, predict_taken=1`. Expected next state is `32'h0000_0002`; the prediction is ignored.
