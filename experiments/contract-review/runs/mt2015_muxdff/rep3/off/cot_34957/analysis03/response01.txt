The public spec describes a three-bit LFSR/load register, `full_module`, that must be rebuilt hierarchically as `top_module` using three instances of a one-bit submodule containing a flip-flop and a 2-to-1 multiplexer. The checker's job is to confirm both the structural decomposition and the cycle-accurate behavior under the seed-20261122 protocol.

Behaviorally, each bit of `q` updates on the rising edge of `clk`. When `L=1`, the bit loads its corresponding `r` bit; when `L=0`, the register shifts according to `q <= {q[1]^q[2], q[0], q[2]}`. In the hierarchical version, the XOR `q[1]^q[2]` cannot live inside a one-bit submodule because it needs two different bits of the state. It must be computed in `top_module` and routed into the `q_in` port of the instance that produces the new `q[2]`. The other two instances receive `q[0]` and `q[2]` as their `q_in` values, matching the shift. Each instance's `r_in` receives the corresponding bit of `r`, and all three share `L` and `clk`. The checker should verify exactly three submodule instantiations and that the feedback net connects to the correct instance.

The timing model is the critical part. The design is purely edge-triggered: no asynchronous paths exist, so row *i*'s sampled `Q` depends only on the state and pre-edge inputs present at row *i*'s posedge. Mid-input changes that occur after sampling but before the next edge are carried forward to row *i+1*'s pre-edge window unless row *i+1* explicitly overrides them. A checker that applies mid-inputs to the current row will mispredict whenever a mid-input differs from the row's pre-edge value. The correct sequence per row is: drive pre-edge inputs, sample after the posedge settles, then apply optional mid-inputs, carrying them into the next row's pre-edge values.

Worked example: row 1 has `L=0`, `r=000`, `q=001` before its posedge; the checker samples `Q` after settling. A mid-input then sets `L=1`. Row 2 begins with `L=1` carried forward unless row 2 drives a new `L`. If row 2 does not override, its posedge performs a parallel load, not an LFSR step; if it overrides, the override wins and the carried value is discarded. The same carry rule applies to `r_in` and `q_in` mid-changes.

For functional comparison, the checker should compute the three-bit next-state function from the pre-edge state and inputs, compare it against the sampled post-edge `Q`, and use carried mid-inputs only for the following row. This separation of sampled-edge semantics from mid-input carry is the single most important modeling decision for matching the seed-20261122 protocol.
