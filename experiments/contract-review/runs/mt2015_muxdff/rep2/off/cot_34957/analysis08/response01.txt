The public module has no reset and no initial value for `q`. At power-up, `q` is therefore unknown (`3'bxxx` in simulation). The checker must not assume a known starting state. Two safe strategies are: (1) begin with an `L=1` row so the first sampled edge loads `q <= r` and establishes a known state; or (2) treat all outputs sampled before the first `L=1` row as don't-care. Any row with `L=0` before establishment will propagate unknowns through the XOR and shift, so the reference model must also produce `x` there, or the checker must skip comparison until establishment.

The submodule structure is a flipflop plus a 2-to-1 mux, instantiated three times. Each instance holds one bit of `q`. The mux selects `r_in` when `L=1` and the combinational feedback when `L=0`. The feedback wiring is the critical detail: the MSB instance's data input is `q[1] ^ q[2]`, the middle instance's is `q[0]`, and the LSB instance's is `q[2]`. These are the *current* (pre-edge) values of `q`, sampled combinationally before the clock edge. A checker that validates against the public recurrence `q_next = {q[1]^q[2], q[0], q[2]}` will catch any miswiring, including the tempting same-index error.

Worked example: after an `L=1` row loads `q = 3'b110`, drive `L=0`. Correct next state is MSB `1^0=1`, middle `q[0]=0`, LSB `q[2]=0`, giving `Q=3'b100`. A same-index miswiring would give MSB `q[1]^q[1]=0`, middle `0`, LSB `0`, i.e. `000`. One such row distinguishes the designs; the checker should include several states with `q[1] != q[2]` to exercise the XOR in both polarities.

Timing: the design is edge-triggered on `posedge clk`. The checker drives `L` and `r` before the sampled edge, waits for the posedge to settle, then samples `Q`. Optional mid-cycle changes to `L` or `r` occur after sampling and before the next edge; they are carried forward as the next row's inputs and must not affect the just-sampled `Q`. Because the submodule flipflops are also edge-triggered, the feedback path is stable at the edge, so the sampled `Q` reflects the pre-edge `q` and the current row's `L`/`r`.

The useful point for the final Python checker: implement the reference model as the recurrence above, seeded with `x` for `q`, and compare bit-for-bit only after establishment. Do not infer expected behavior from the submodule netlist, and do not assume a reset value. Include at least one `L=0` row immediately after a known load with `q[1] != q[2]`, plus a mid-cycle input change that must be carried to the next row without altering the current sample.
