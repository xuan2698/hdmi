The public module `full_module` is a 3-bit register with a synchronous load enable. Port widths are fixed: `r` is 3 bits, `L` is 1 bit, `clk` is 1 bit, and `q` is a 3-bit registered output. The submodule `top_module` must expose a 1-bit `L`, a 1-bit `r_in`, a 1-bit `q_in`, a 1-bit `Q`, and `clk`. Three instances of `top_module` are wired together in the parent to reconstruct the 3-bit behavior: each instance holds one bit of `q`, receives the corresponding bit of `r` as `r_in`, and receives the appropriate feedback bit as `q_in`. The parent must combine the three `Q` outputs into the 3-bit `q`.

Boundary values matter. Because each submodule is 1 bit wide, `r_in`, `q_in`, and `Q` can only be `0`, `1`, `x`, or `z`. The parent's 3-bit `r` and `q` are formed by concatenating three such bits. The load condition `L` is shared across all three instances, so all three bits must load simultaneously. The recurrence `q <= {q[1] ^ q[2], q[0], q[2]}` is a bit permutation plus one XOR. In the hierarchical design, the XOR must be computed from the current `q[1]` and `q[2]` outputs, not from delayed or registered copies. The submodule's `q_in` is the feedback input for that bit; the parent routes `q[1] ^ q[2]` into the instance that produces the new `q[2]` (the MSB), `q[0]` into the instance that produces the new `q[1]`, and `q[2]` into the instance that produces the new `q[0]`. The exact instance-to-bit mapping is an implementation choice, but the public behavior must match the recurrence.

The checker should model the top-level `q` as unknown until the first sampled edge with `L=1`. At that edge, `q` must equal `r` exactly. After that, every `L=0` edge must apply the recurrence to the previously sampled `q`. Mid-cycle changes to `L` or `r` occur after sampling and must not affect the just-sampled `q`; only the next edge sees them. A combinational leak from `L` or `r` into `q` would violate this.

A useful point for the final Python checker: do not assume any particular power-up value, and do not compare pre-synchronization samples. Seed the model as unknown, overwrite with `r` at the first `L=1` sample, then apply the recurrence. Also ensure at least one `L=0` step after a load where `q[1] != q[2]`, because loads of `000` or `111` can mask XOR-polarity bugs. For example, load `r=101` with `L=1`, giving `q=101`; then with `L=0` the correct next state is `{0^1, 1, 1} = 110`. A design that permutes without the XOR would give `101`, which differs. The checker must derive expected `q` solely from the public recurrence and the sampled edge timing, with no extra latency from parent-level registers.
