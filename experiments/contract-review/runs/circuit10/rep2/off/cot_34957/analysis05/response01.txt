The public table is best read as a parity machine with one stored bit, but the clocked column is not globally self-consistent. The within-row relation is the reliable part: for every row where `state` and `q` are defined, `q` equals `a XOR b XOR state`. Check this first. It holds at 15 ns (`0^0^0=0`), at 45 ns (`0^1^0=1`), at 65 ns (`1^1^0=0`), at 75 ns (`0^0^1=1`), at 95 ns (`1^1^1=1`), at 115 ns (`1^0^1=0`), and at 135 ns (`0^0^1=1`). This is the strongest invariant in the spec and should be the primary assertion.

The clocked behavior is messier. The natural intent is `state_next = q_prev` on the rising edge, and several transitions fit: 75→85 ns has `q=1` at 75 and `state=0` at 85? No—that one does not fit either. Actually 75 ns `q=1`, 85 ns `state=0`, so it fails. 95→105 ns has `q=1` at 95 and `state=1` at 105, which fits. 115→125 ns has `q=0` at 115 and `state=1` at 125, which fails. 135→145 ns has `q=1` at 135 and `state=0` at 145, which fails. So the table cannot be enforcing `state_next = q_prev` uniformly. A checker should not hard-fail on that relation globally. At most, it can flag it as a soft or conditional check where adjacent defined rows happen to agree.

The all-zero input runs are the next useful discriminator. From 10–40 ns, `a=0,b=0` and both `state` and `q` stay 0. From 145–190 ns, again `a=0,b=0` and both stay 0. This rules out a free-running toggle, a counter, or any autonomous state evolution. With both inputs low, the machine holds. It also argues against a synchronous reset encoded in the inputs, since no reset-like transition appears in those windows.

The initial rows at 0–10 ns show `x` in `state` and `q`. Treat that as an uninitialized flip-flop at power-up, not as a deliberate reset value. The first defined sampled state is 0 at 15 ns. A checker should skip rows with `x` and begin checking once a row supplies a defined `state`, rather than assuming a power-on value.

A short worked example: at 45 ns, `a=0,b=1,state=0`, so `q = 0^1^0 = 1`, matching the table. At 55 ns, `a=1,b=0,state=0`, so `q = 1^0^0 = 1`, matching. The state column at 55 ns is still 0, which is consistent with the previous sampled `q` only if the update is not enforced strictly; this is exactly the kind of row where a global `state_next=q_prev` check would produce a false failure.

So the final checker should: skip `x` rows; assert `q == a ^ b ^ state` on every defined row; assert that `a=0,b=0` leaves `state` unchanged across a rising edge; and treat `state_next == q_prev` as a conditional or soft check only where the table is self-consistent. The initial `x` region is uninitialized power-up, not a reset contract.
