The waveform is best read as a one-bit Moore machine whose visible `state` is the flip-flop contents and whose `q` is the combinational next-state/output function. The long stable stretches are the key evidence. From 15–40 ns, with `a=0` and `b=0`, both `state` and `q` remain 0. From 145–190 ns, again with `a=0` and `b=0`, both remain 0. Those runs rule out a free-running toggle, a counter, or any autonomous state change: with both inputs low the machine holds. They also argue against a synchronous reset encoded in the inputs, because no reset-like transition appears in those windows.

The first rows at 0–10 ns show `x` in `state` and `q`. That is most naturally modeled as an uninitialized flip-flop at power-up, not as a deliberate reset state. The first defined sampled state appears at 15 ns as 0. A checker should therefore treat the initial `x` region as unknown and begin checking once a row supplies a defined `state`, rather than assuming a particular power-on value.

Within a row, the combinational relation that fits the public data is `q = a ^ b ^ state`. This is the useful invariant for the checker: it ties the observable output to the current inputs and the current flip-flop contents without depending on the next row. The clocked part is then `state <= q` at the rising edge, so the next sampled `state` should equal the previous row’s `q` when the waveform is internally consistent.

A short worked example: at 45 ns the row has `a=0`, `b=1`, `state=0`, `q=1`. The XOR relation gives `0 ^ 1 ^ 0 = 1`, matching `q`. At the next rising edge, 55 ns, the table shows `state=0`, which would require the previous `q` to have been 0, not 1. That is one of the places where the public rows are not internally consistent as a simple pre-edge/post-edge table. The checker should not overfit such conflicts. Instead it should prefer the within-row XOR relation and the hold-under-`a=b=0` behavior, and use the clocked update only where consecutive rows agree.

The practical checker strategy is therefore: skip rows with `x`; for each defined row verify `q == a ^ b ^ state`; verify that when `a=0` and `b=0` the state does not change across a rising edge; and verify `state_next == q` only on transitions where the table is self-consistent. Treat the initial `x` rows as uninitialized power-up, not as a reset contract.
