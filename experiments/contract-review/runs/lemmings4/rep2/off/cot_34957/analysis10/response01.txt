The interface is entirely one-bit: inputs `areset`, `bump_left`, `bump_right`, `ground`, and `dig`; outputs `walk_left`, `walk_right`, `aaah`, and `digging`. There are no multi-bit ports, so no width-truncation or signedness issues arise. The only numeric boundary is the fall-duration threshold: “more than 20 clock cycles” means a fall counter must distinguish 20 from 21. A fall of exactly 20 cycles followed by ground contact must not splatter; 21 must. The checker should therefore model the counter as an integer with an explicit `> 20` test at the ground-contact transition, not as a saturating flag that could blur the boundary.

The Moore outputs are mutually constrained by state. Walking states assert exactly one of `walk_left`/`walk_right`; falling asserts `aaah`; digging asserts `digging`. Splattered is the all-zero output condition. Because outputs are decoded from state, mid-cycle input changes must not alter the current sampled output row; they only affect the next sampled edge.

Reset is asynchronous and positive-edge triggered, and it dominates all ordinary transitions. It must force the walk-left state, clear the fall counter, and clear the splatter memory. The splatter condition is permanent “until reset,” so reset must clear that memory rather than merely restarting direction. The fall counter must also clear so a pre-reset long fall cannot contaminate post-reset behavior.

For ordinary transitions, precedence is fall, then dig, then bump. If `ground=0` while walking or digging, enter falling and assert `aaah`. While falling, bumps and `dig` are ignored. On `ground=1` return, resume the remembered walking direction unless the fall exceeded 20 cycles, in which case splatter. The splatter check occurs only on ground contact, never mid-air. A walking Lemming on ground with `dig=1` starts digging; otherwise either bump switches direction, including simultaneous bumps. Digging continues until `ground=0`, then falls, then resumes the original direction after landing.

A short worked example: suppose the Lemming is falling with fall count 21 and `areset` asserts before the next sampled edge. The correct next sampled row is walk-left with fall and splatter memory cleared. The checker must not first process a ground-contact splatter transition and then reset; reset dominates and directly establishes walk-left.

The final Python checker should separate three concerns: asynchronous reset override, ordinary next-state computation with fall/dig/bump precedence, and Moore output decoding from the resolved state. Mid-cycle input changes should be carried to the next sampled edge but must not retroactively alter the current output row. This separation guards against incorrect fall-count preservation, splatter persistence across reset, and direction loss during fall or dig.
