The most useful thing for the final checker is to treat the fall counter and the splatter latch as the two pieces of hidden state that must be verified independently of the visible walking direction. The public spec makes the counter's boundary explicit: "more than 20 clock cycles" means the landing test is `count > 20`, not `count >= 20`. A checker that only exercises short falls will never distinguish a correct implementation from one that splatters at 20 or that never splatters at all. The distinguishing input should therefore drive a fall of exactly 20 cycles, land, and confirm safe resumption; then drive a fall of 21 cycles, land, and confirm the sticky splatter output. Because the counter is not an output, the checker must infer it from behavior across a landing edge, and it should also confirm that the counter is cleared on a safe landing so that a subsequent short fall does not inherit the old count.

The second useful point is precedence. The spec orders fall > dig > direction switch. A cycle with `ground=0` and `dig=1` must produce falling behavior (`aaah=1`, `digging=0`), not digging. A cycle with `ground=1`, `dig=1`, and a bump must produce digging, not a direction change. And a simultaneous `bump_left=1` and `bump_right=1` must still flip direction, so the checker should not treat a double bump as a no-op. These three cases are cheap to drive and catch the most common precedence mistakes.

The third point is reset. `areset` is positive-edge-triggered asynchronous, so it must act on its rising edge without waiting for `clk`. Reset must restore walk-left, clear falling, digging, and splattered, and zero the fall counter. The classic bug is a surviving counter or splatter latch: reset during a long fall, then a short fall, must land safely rather than splatter from pre-reset history. The checker should assert reset mid-fall and then verify the next sampled row shows walk-left with a zero counter, and that a subsequent 21-cycle fall still splatters (proving the latch was cleared, not merely masked).

Worked example: hold `ground=0` for 20 sampled cycles, then set `ground=1`. The correct output is a safe landing: `aaah` drops, the prior direction resumes, and the counter clears. If the same sequence is extended to 21 cycles before `ground=1`, the correct output is splatter: all four outputs become 0 and stay 0 until reset. A reset asserted during either fall must behave as if no fall occurred, so the next sampled row is walk-left with a zero counter.

Finally, the checker should sample after the posedge has settled, not at the instant of the edge, and should carry mid-cycle input changes to the start of the next row. That timing discipline matters most around the landing edge, where the counter comparison and the splatter latch update together.
