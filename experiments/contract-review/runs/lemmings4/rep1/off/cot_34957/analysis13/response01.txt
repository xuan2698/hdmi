The key timing point for the final checker is the distinction between the sample that *starts* a fall and the sample that *ends* it. Because this is a Moore machine sampled after the posedge settles, the outputs and next-state decision for a row are based on the inputs present at that sampled edge. A fall begins on the first sampled cycle where `ground=0` while the machine was walking or digging on ground; that same sample already shows `aaah=1` and no walking/digging output. The fall duration should therefore be counted in terms of sampled falling cycles, and the landing sample is the first sample with `ground=1` while the machine is in the falling state. On that landing sample, the machine is still in the falling state for output purposes unless the design deliberately makes landing and resumption simultaneous; the public behavior says it resumes walking once it hits ground again, so the checker should be clear whether the landing sample itself outputs walking or still outputs `aaah`. The safest reading of the spec is that the landing sample is the transition sample: the fall has ended, so the machine resumes walking in the stored direction, and the splatter decision is made using the accumulated fall length at that moment.

The splatter threshold is the most error-prone part. The spec says “more than 20 clock cycles,” so a fall of exactly 20 cycles must not splatter. The checker should therefore use a strict `> 20` comparison on the landing sample, not `>= 20`, and it should not increment the counter one extra time merely because the landing sample is observed. A natural implementation stores the fall count as the number of completed falling samples before landing; if the counter is incremented on every falling sample including the landing sample, then a 20-cycle fall becomes 21 and splatters incorrectly. The checker should test both the boundary case of exactly 20 falling samples followed by ground and the case of 21 falling samples followed by ground.

Direction storage must be independent of bumps during fall and digging. A bump while falling does not change the stored walking direction, and a bump on the same sampled cycle as landing also does not change the resumed direction. Similarly, `dig=1` on the landing sample is ignored because the machine is still resolving the fall; digging can only begin on a later sample when the machine is walking on ground. The checker should include sequences where `bump_left` or `bump_right` is asserted during the fall and on the landing edge, then verify that the resumed direction matches the pre-fall direction.

The Moore-output invariant is a strong checker aid: in every non-splattered sample exactly one of `walk_left`, `walk_right`, `aaah`, and `digging` is 1; in the splattered state all four are 0. This catches encoding mistakes around digging-to-falling and falling-to-walking transitions. The checker should also verify that splatter is absorbing until `areset`, and that asynchronous positive-edge reset immediately forces the walk-left state regardless of current state or inputs. Finally, the fall counter must be cleared when walking resumes, so a later fall starts from zero rather than inheriting the previous duration.
