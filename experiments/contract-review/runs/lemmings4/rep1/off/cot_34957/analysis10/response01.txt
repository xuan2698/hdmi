The interface is a single clocked Moore machine with five 1-bit inputs (`areset`, `bump_left`, `bump_right`, `ground`, `dig`) and four 1-bit outputs (`walk_left`, `walk_right`, `aaah`, `digging`). All ports are scalar; there are no multi-bit buses, so no width-truncation or signedness issues arise. Every input is sampled as a Boolean, and every output is a Boolean. The only internal quantity with a range is the fall-duration counter, which must distinguish 0 through 20 from 21-or-more; a 5-bit counter (0–31) suffices, with saturation or a “too long” flag once it exceeds 20. Because the specification speaks of “more than 20 clock cycles,” the boundary is exact: a fall of 20 sampled `ground=0` cycles is survivable, while 21 is fatal.

Reset is asynchronous and positive-edge triggered, with highest priority. On `areset`, the machine returns to walking left: `walk_left=1`, the other three outputs 0, direction stored as left, fall counter cleared, digging flag cleared, splattered flag cleared. Reset must also clear any “falling” or “digging” mode so that a subsequent sequence starts cleanly. Since reset is asynchronous, the checker should allow the reset state to appear without waiting for a clock edge, but all other transitions are evaluated on the rising edge after inputs have settled.

The outputs are Moore outputs, so they depend only on the current state, not directly on the current inputs. In every non-splattered state exactly one of `walk_left`, `walk_right`, `aaah`, `digging` is high. In the splattered state all four are 0. This mutual exclusivity is a useful invariant for the checker: it can reject any sample where two or more of the four outputs are simultaneously 1, or where all four are 0 without the machine being splattered.

Direction is part of the walking state. A walking Lemming on ground with `dig=0` keeps its direction unless bumped. `bump_left=1` forces rightward walking, `bump_right=1` forces leftward walking, and both together still cause a switch. Bumps are ignored while falling or digging. If `ground=0` while walking, the machine enters falling (`aaah=1`) and preserves direction; if `dig=1` while walking on ground, it enters digging (`digging=1`) and preserves direction. Precedence is fall over dig over bump.

Digging lasts while `ground=1`; when `ground=0`, digging ends and falling begins, preserving direction. A `dig=1` while falling or with `ground=0` is ignored. Falling increments the duration each cycle `ground=0`. On the cycle `ground` returns to 1, if the accumulated duration exceeds 20 the machine splatters permanently; otherwise it resumes walking in the stored direction and clears the counter. Bumps during falling, and bumps in the same cycle ground disappears or reappears, do not change direction.

Worked example: walking left, then `ground=0` for 21 sampled cycles, then `ground=1`. During the fall `aaah=1` and direction remains left. On the landing sample the duration is 21, so all outputs become 0 and stay 0 until reset. A 20-cycle fall instead resumes `walk_left=1`.

For the final checker, the critical points are reset priority and counter clearing, the exact 20-versus-21 landing boundary, direction preservation across fall and dig, bump suppression during fall and dig, and the absorbing nature of splatter.
