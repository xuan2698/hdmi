One useful implementation mistake to target in the final checker is **off-by-one fall-duration counting combined with incorrect splatter timing**. The spec says a Lemming splatters if it falls for *more than 20* clock cycles and then hits the ground. A natural but wrong implementation increments the fall counter on the landing cycle as well, or checks the counter before deciding whether the landing sample is still "falling." That shifts the survival boundary by one cycle: a 20-cycle fall may splatter, or a 21-cycle fall may survive. The checker should therefore construct two sequences that differ only in the length of the zero run, and verify survival versus splatter.

A short worked example clarifies the counting. Suppose the machine is walking right on ground. The checker drives `ground=0` for 20 consecutive samples, then `ground=1`. On the first zero sample the machine transitions to falling (`aaah=1`); that sample is fall cycle 1. The twentieth zero sample is fall cycle 20. The next sample has `ground=1`, so the machine lands; since the fall lasted 20 cycles (not more than 20), it resumes walking right with `aaah=0`. If instead there were 21 zero samples, the landing sample would splatter and all outputs would go to 0 permanently.

Direction latching is the other boundary the checker must pin down. The pre-fall direction must be preserved across the fall and restored on landing, and bumps during falling, on the ground-disappearance sample, and on the landing sample must not flip it. The checker should therefore include a walking-right state, drop ground with `bump_left=1` simultaneously, fall a few cycles, then land with `bump_right=1`, and confirm the resumed direction is still right. This distinguishes correct gating (bump logic only in the walking-and-grounded state) from an implementation that applies bump unconditionally.

Finally, the splattered state is absorbing and must be tested for persistence under arbitrary inputs, and reset must clear both direction and the fall counter. The checker should splatter, hold all inputs high for several cycles confirming all-zero outputs, assert `areset`, and confirm the next sample is `walk_left=1`. Then a fresh 15-cycle fall and landing must not splatter, proving the counter was cleared rather than merely the direction. Reset is asynchronous and positive-edge triggered, so the checker must assert it early enough that the post-reset sample already reflects walk-left; asserting after the sampled edge produces a one-cycle lag and false mismatches. A reset-then-immediate-bump case (deassert reset before the next sampled edge with `bump_right=1`) confirms no residual state survives.
