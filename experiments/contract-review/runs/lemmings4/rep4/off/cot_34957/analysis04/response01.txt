The interface is deliberately narrow: five one-bit inputs (`areset`, `bump_left`, `bump_right`, `ground`, `dig`) and four one-bit Moore outputs (`walk_left`, `walk_right`, `aaah`, `digging`). Because every signal is scalar, there are no multi-bit boundary values to interpret; the only numeric boundary that matters is the fall-duration threshold of 20 cycles. The checker should therefore treat all input combinations as legal binary vectors and concentrate on state history rather than on encoding width.

The output encoding is one-hot in intent but not guaranteed mutually exclusive by the prose alone: walking left, walking right, falling, and digging are described as distinct modes, and splattering forces all four outputs to zero. A useful checker should verify that exactly one of `walk_left`, `walk_right`, `aaah`, and `digging` is asserted in every non-splattered, non-reset cycle, and that all four are zero in the splattered state. Reset is positive-edge-triggered asynchronous and forces the walk-left state, so the checker must allow outputs to change without waiting for `clk` when `areset` rises.

The fall counter is the main boundary-sensitive element. It must count cycles spent falling, be cleared whenever the machine is not falling, and be evaluated only on the landing transition. The phrase “more than 20 clock cycles” means a fall of exactly 20 cycles is survivable; 21 or more splatters. The checker should therefore test the values 19, 20, 21, and a much larger count, and should confirm that splattering is decided only when `ground=1` arrives while falling, never in mid-air. Once splattered, all outputs remain zero forever until reset.

Precedence is another checker focus. On an ordinary sampled edge, loss of ground outranks digging, and digging outranks bump-induced direction changes. A bump in the same cycle that ground disappears must not alter the remembered direction, and a bump in the same cycle that ground reappears while still falling must not alter resumption direction. While falling, both bumps and `dig` are ignored. While digging, bumps are ignored, and digging continues until `ground=0`, at which point the machine falls and later resumes walking in the direction remembered from before the dig.

A short worked example: suppose the machine is walking right, has been falling for 25 cycles, and `areset` rises mid-cycle. The asynchronous reset must immediately produce `walk_left=1` with `walk_right=0`, `aaah=0`, and `digging=0`, and it must erase the fall count. On the next sampled edge with `ground=1`, no bump, and no dig, the machine remains walking left; the earlier 25-cycle fall must not cause splattering because reset cleared the history.
