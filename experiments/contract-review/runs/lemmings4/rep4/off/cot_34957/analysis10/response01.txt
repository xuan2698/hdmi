The port widths are all one bit: inputs `areset`, `bump_left`, `bump_right`, `ground`, and `dig`; outputs `walk_left`, `walk_right`, `aaah`, and `digging`. Because every signal is scalar, the checker should treat each sampled row as a Boolean vector and avoid any multi-bit packing assumptions. The only internal quantity with a range is the fall-duration counter, which must distinguish 0 through 20 from 21 and above. That counter is not a port, but its boundary behavior is the main hidden state the checker must probe.

The boundary values that matter are therefore not numeric port values but cycle counts and precedence combinations. For fall duration, the critical thresholds are 19, 20, 21, and a large value. Exactly 20 falling cycles followed by `ground=1` must survive; 21 must splatter. The counter must be cleared whenever the machine is not falling, and it must be cleared by `areset`. A design that merely freezes the counter during walking, or that fails to clear it on reset, can pass isolated falls but fail when falls are interrupted or repeated.

Reset is asynchronous and positive-edge triggered. The checker should apply `areset` between sampled edges and verify that the outputs and the fall history immediately reflect the reset state: walking left, not right, not aaah, not digging, with the fall counter erased. A useful case is a long fall interrupted by reset and then followed by a normal landing. The pre-reset fall duration must not contribute to splattering.

A short worked example: suppose the machine is walking right and has fallen for 25 cycles. `areset` rises mid-cycle. Immediately, `walk_left=1`, `walk_right=0`, `aaah=0`, `digging=0`, and the fall counter is zero. On the next sampled edge with `ground=1`, no bump, and no dig, the machine remains walking left. The earlier 25-cycle fall must not cause splattering. This checks both asynchronous reset and counter clearing.

Precedence is the other focus. On a normal sampled edge, loss of ground outranks digging, and digging outranks bump-induced direction changes. A bump in the same cycle that ground disappears must not change the remembered walking direction. A bump in the same cycle that ground reappears while still falling must not change the resumption direction. While falling, bumps and `dig` are ignored. While digging, bumps are ignored, and digging continues until `ground=0`; then the machine falls and later resumes walking in the direction remembered from before the dig.

The checker should also confirm that exactly one of `walk_left`, `walk_right`, `aaah`, and `digging` is asserted in every non-splattered cycle, and that all four are zero after splattering. Splattering is decided only on the landing transition, when `ground=1` arrives while falling with a fall duration greater than 20; it must never occur in mid-air. Once splattered, all outputs remain zero until reset.
