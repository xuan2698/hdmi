The key timing point for the checker is that direction is *state*, not an output. During `aaah=1` or `digging=1`, `walk_left`/`walk_right` are both low, so the checker cannot infer direction from outputs; it must remember the pre-fall/pre-dig direction and verify it after resumption. Bumps must be ignored on exactly three edge classes: the edge where `ground` drops (still walking), every mid-fall edge, and the edge where `ground` returns while still falling. Asserting both bumps across those edges and checking the resumed walking output equals the pre-fall direction is the sharpest probe for a leaky direction register.

Worked example: walking right, drive `ground=1, dig=1, bump_left=1` → `digging=1` only, direction still right (dig outranks bump). Next edge `ground=0` → `aaah=1`, direction still right (fall outranks dig). Hold `ground=0` ~5 edges with both bumps → no change, no splatter. Then `ground=1` with both bumps → `walk_right=1` alone. This exercises dig-over-bump, fall-over-dig, bump-ignored-in-fall, bump-ignored-on-landing, and the output invariant together.

Also check: exactly one output high per non-splattered edge; all four zero post-splatter until reset; splatter only on landing after >20 fall cycles; async reset effective immediately, next sampled edge shows `walk_left=1` alone.
