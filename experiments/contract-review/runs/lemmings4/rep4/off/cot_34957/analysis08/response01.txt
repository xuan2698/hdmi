The reset behavior is the cleanest place to anchor the checker, because it is the only mechanism that can erase a splatter or an in-progress fall. `areset` is positive-edge-triggered and asynchronous, so the checker must not wait for a clock edge to observe its effect: as soon as `areset` goes high, the outputs must become `walk_left=1`, `walk_right=0`, `aaah=0`, `digging=0`, and the internal fall counter must be cleared. The phrase "positive edge triggered asynchronous" is slightly unusual, but the intended reading is that the reset is level-sensitive in effect (assertion immediately forces the walk-left state) while being described in edge terms for the testbench; the checker should sample after the posedge has settled and treat any high `areset` as authoritative. Power-up is not separately specified, so the checker should not assume a particular initial state before the first reset; it should drive `areset` first and only then begin comparing outputs. This avoids false failures from an uninitialized state.

The most useful single point for the final Python checker is the interaction between reset and the fall counter. The counter must be cleared by reset, not merely frozen or decremented. A design that increments on every `ground=0` cycle, or that forgets to clear on reset, can survive simple falls but fail on interrupted or repeated falls. The checker should therefore include a case where a fall is interrupted by reset and then followed by a normal landing, confirming that the pre-reset fall duration does not contribute to splattering.

A short worked example: the machine is walking right and has been falling for 25 cycles. `areset` rises mid-cycle. Immediately, `walk_left=1`, `walk_right=0`, `aaah=0`, `digging=0`, and the fall counter is zero. On the next sampled edge with `ground=1`, no bump, and no dig, the machine stays walking left. The 25-cycle fall must not cause splattering, because reset erased the history. This checks both asynchronous reset and counter clearing.

Beyond reset, the checker should verify that exactly one of `walk_left`, `walk_right`, `aaah`, and `digging` is asserted in every non-splattered cycle, and that all four are zero in the splattered state. The fall counter must count cycles spent falling, be cleared whenever the machine is not falling, and be evaluated only on the landing transition. "More than 20 clock cycles" means exactly 20 is survivable and 21 splatters; the checker should test 19, 20, 21, and a much larger count, and confirm splattering is decided only when `ground=1` arrives while falling, never in mid-air. Once splattered, all outputs stay zero until reset.

Precedence is the other focus. On an ordinary sampled edge, loss of ground outranks digging, and digging outranks bump-induced direction changes. A bump in the same cycle ground disappears must not alter the remembered direction, and a bump in the same cycle ground reappears while still falling must not alter resumption direction. While falling, bumps and `dig` are ignored. While digging, bumps are ignored, and digging continues until `ground=0`, at which point the machine falls and later resumes walking in the direction remembered from before the dig.
