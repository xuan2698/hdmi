The key distinguishing point for the checker is the interaction between the fall counter, reset, and the "remembered direction" register. These are the two pieces of hidden state that a superficially correct Moore machine can get wrong while still passing simple walk/bump/fall/dig sequences.

Consider the fall counter first. The spec says a Lemming splatters if it falls for *more than 20* clock cycles and then hits ground. The counter must increment only while falling, must be cleared whenever the machine is not falling, and must be cleared by `areset`. A design that freezes the counter during walking, or that fails to clear it on reset, can pass isolated falls but fail when falls are interrupted or repeated. The checker should therefore drive a long fall, interrupt it with reset, and then land normally: the pre-reset fall duration must not contribute to splattering. Symmetrically, a fall of exactly 20 cycles followed by landing must *not* splatter, while 21 must. The splatter decision is made only on the landing transition (ground=1 while falling); mid-air cycles never splatter, and once splattered all four outputs stay zero until reset.

The remembered direction is the second trap. When ground disappears, the machine must latch the current walking direction and resume it on landing, regardless of bumps received during the fall or on the disappearance/reappearance edges. A bump in the same cycle that ground goes to 0 must not alter the latched direction; a bump in the same cycle that ground returns while still falling must not alter the resumption direction. Digging similarly preserves the pre-dig direction: the Lemming digs until ground=0, falls, then resumes walking the original way.

Precedence on a normal sampled edge is: fall (ground=0) over dig over bump. So if ground=0 and dig=1 and a bump arrives together, the machine falls, ignores the dig, and ignores the bump for direction purposes. While falling, both bump and dig are ignored. While digging, bumps are ignored and digging persists until ground=0.

Worked example: walking right, ground=1, dig=1, bump_left=1 on the same edge. Dig outranks bump, so the machine enters digging and keeps walking-right memory; the bump is discarded. Next edge ground=0: digging ends, machine falls, aaah=1, direction memory still right. Several falling edges with bump_right=1 change nothing. Then ground=1 with fall count ≤20: machine resumes walk_right=1. The checker should verify the direction never flipped and that exactly one of the four outputs was asserted throughout, with all zero only after a genuine >20-cycle landing splatter.
