The key timing point for the checker is that the Moore outputs are a function of the registered state alone, so they must be sampled after the posedge has settled and must not depend on the current-cycle inputs. Inputs presented before a sampled edge determine the next state; mid-cycle input changes after sampling affect only the following edge. Asynchronous reset is the exception: a rising `areset` must take effect immediately, not at the next clock edge, forcing the walk-left state and clearing the fall counter.

For the fall counter, the checker should distinguish “cycles spent falling” from “cycles since ground was lost.” The counter should advance only while the machine is actually in the falling state, should be cleared whenever the machine is not falling, and should be consulted only on the landing transition—when `ground=1` arrives while the state is falling. A design that increments whenever `ground=0`, or that fails to clear on reset, can pass simple falls but fail on interrupted or repeated falls.

A useful distinguishing case is a fall interrupted by reset. Suppose the machine is walking right and has been falling for 25 cycles. If `areset` rises mid-cycle, the asynchronous reset must immediately force the walk-left state: `walk_left=1`, `walk_right=0`, `aaah=0`, `digging=0`. It must also clear the fall counter. On the next sampled edge with `ground=1`, no bump, and no dig, the machine should remain walking left. The earlier 25-cycle fall must not cause splattering, because reset erased the history. This example checks both the asynchronous reset behavior and the fall-counter clearing rule.

The checker should also verify that exactly one of `walk_left`, `walk_right`, `aaah`, and `digging` is asserted in every non-splattered, non-reset cycle, and that all four are zero in the splattered state. The fall counter must count cycles spent falling, be cleared whenever the machine is not falling, and be evaluated only on the landing transition. The phrase “more than 20 clock cycles” means a fall of exactly 20 cycles is survivable; 21 or more splatters. The checker should test the values 19, 20, 21, and a much larger count, and should confirm that splattering is decided only when `ground=1` arrives while falling, never in mid-air. Once splattered, all outputs remain zero forever until reset.

Precedence is another checker focus. On an ordinary sampled edge, loss of ground outranks digging, and digging outranks bump-induced direction changes. A bump in the same cycle that ground disappears must not alter the remembered direction, and a bump in the same cycle that ground reappears while still falling must not alter resumption direction. While falling, both bumps and `dig` are ignored. While digging, bumps are ignored, and digging continues until `ground=0`, at which point the machine falls and later resumes walking in the direction remembered from before the dig.
