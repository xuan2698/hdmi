The key timing point for the checker is that the Moore outputs are sampled after the posedge has settled, so every row’s inputs are already stable before the edge that produces that row’s outputs. Mid-cycle input changes occur after sampling and before the opposite edge, so they must be treated as setup for the next sampled row, not as retroactive changes to the current outputs. In practice, the checker should compare outputs only after the active edge, and should not expect a mid-input change to alter the just-sampled Moore state until the following clock edge.

For the fall-counter boundary, the important distinction is between “falling for exactly 20 sampled cycles” and “falling for more than 20.” A Lemming that is airborne for 20 cycles and then lands must survive and resume walking in the remembered direction. A Lemming airborne for 21 cycles and then landing must splatter permanently. This means the checker must count sampled cycles in which `aaah=1` is the post-edge output, not merely cycles in which `ground=0` was driven. The landing edge itself is where the splatter decision is made, so an implementation that increments the fall counter on the landing edge instead of only while falling can incorrectly splatter at 20 or fail to splatter at 21.

A short worked example: start from reset with `walk_left=1`. Drive `ground=0` for 20 consecutive sampled cycles. During those rows, `aaah` should become 1 and both walking outputs 0. Then drive `ground=1`. On the post-edge sample after that landing row, the expected outputs are `walk_left=1`, `walk_right=0`, `aaah=0`, `digging=0`. If the checker sees all-zero outputs here, the implementation has treated 20 fall cycles as fatal. Repeating the same sequence with 21 fall cycles should instead yield all-zero outputs forever, including after later `ground=1` and `dig=1` inputs.

The checker should also verify that splatter is permanent until `areset`. After a 21-cycle fall and landing, drive `ground=1`, `dig=1`, and alternating bumps; all four outputs must remain zero. Then assert `areset=1` and confirm return to `walk_left=1` with the other outputs zero. This proves reset clears the splatter flag and reinitializes the remembered direction.

Two direction-memory traps deserve direct tests. First, from walk-left, assert `ground=0` and `bump_right=1` on the same row. The bump must not change the remembered direction because ground is disappearing. Restore ground within the safe fall window and verify the Lemming resumes walking left. Second, repeat at the landing edge: while `aaah` was previously high, assert `ground=1` and `bump_right=1` together. The bump must not affect direction on the landing cycle; the Lemming should resume its pre-fall direction.

Finally, test precedence directly. From walk-left on ground, assert `dig=1` and `bump_right=1`; expected post-edge outputs are `digging=1`, `walk_left=0`, `walk_right=0`, `aaah=0`. Then assert `ground=0` while digging; expected outputs are `aaah=1`, `digging=0`, and both walking outputs zero. After ground returns within 20 cycles, the Lemming must resume walking left, not right. This catches implementations that let bumping override digging or that forget the remembered direction across a dig-then-fall sequence.
