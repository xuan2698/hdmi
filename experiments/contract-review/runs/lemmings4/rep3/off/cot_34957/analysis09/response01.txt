The key point for the final Python checker is that `areset` is the only defined initialization mechanism, and it must be treated as an asynchronous, highest-priority override of every other input and every internal state. The checker should not assume any particular power-up state; if a test does not drive `areset`, it must either avoid depending on the initial outputs or require the testbench to issue a reset first. This is essential because the Lemming's remembered walking direction during a fall, and its permanent splattered condition, both depend on a well-defined starting state.

The checker should separate the reset path from the normal transition path. On a positive edge of `areset`, the expected outputs are unconditionally `walk_left=1`, `walk_right=0`, `aaah=0`, `digging=0`, regardless of the current state—including the splattered state, the falling state, and the digging state. Simultaneous `bump_left`, `bump_right`, `ground=0`, or `dig=1` on the reset edge must not alter the reset result; reset has priority. The checker should also verify that after reset is released, the machine begins walking left and that the fall counter and splatter flag are cleared, so that subsequent fall-duration and splatter tests are anchored to a known walk-left starting point.

For the normal transition path, the checker should reason in terms of precedence: falling has higher precedence than digging, which has higher precedence than direction switching. A walking Lemming on ground can fall, dig, or switch directions; if more than one condition is satisfied, fall wins over dig, and dig wins over switching. Being bumped while falling or digging has no effect on the remembered walking direction. Being bumped in the same cycle as ground disappears, or when ground reappears while still falling, also does not affect the walking direction. Digging continues until `ground=0`, at which point the Lemming falls and then resumes walking in its original direction once it hits ground again. A fall lasting more than 20 clock cycles followed by hitting the ground causes splatter, after which all four outputs remain zero forever until reset.

A short worked example: if the machine is splattered with all outputs zero and `areset` rises, the post-reset outputs must be `walk_left=1`, `walk_right=0`, `aaah=0`, `digging=0`. If the same edge also has `bump_right=1`, the checker should still expect walk-left, because reset overrides the bump. After reset, the next sampled edge with `ground=1` and no bumps should continue walking left. This confirms that the checker is not accidentally carrying over the splatter flag or the remembered direction from before reset.

The final checker should therefore make reset the explicit initialization event, verify asynchronous clearing of all state including the fall counter and splatter flag, and avoid assuming any power-up state without a reset. That focus will prevent false failures caused by undefined initial conditions and will keep the splatter and fall-duration tests anchored to a known walk-left starting point.
