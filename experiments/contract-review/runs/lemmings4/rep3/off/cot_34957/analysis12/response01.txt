One likely implementation mistake is treating the splatter condition as “fall counter reaches 20” rather than “falls for more than 20 cycles and then hits ground.” A counter that increments on every falling cycle and compares with `>= 20` at the landing edge will splatter one cycle too early. The public spec is explicit that a fall of exactly 20 clock cycles is survivable; only a fall of 21 or more cycles followed by ground contact causes splatter. This is especially easy to get wrong because the counter may be incremented on the same edge that ground returns, so the landing edge can accidentally count as an extra fall cycle.

A useful checker sequence is: reset to walk-left, drive `ground=0` for exactly 20 sampled cycles, then drive `ground=1`. The expected post-edge outputs are `walk_left=1`, `walk_right=0`, `aaah=0`, `digging=0`. If the implementation splatters here, the counter threshold or the landing-edge increment is wrong. Repeating with 21 fall cycles should instead produce all-zero outputs forever, including after later `ground=1` and `dig=1` inputs.

A related mistake is failing to make splatter permanent. Once splattered, the machine must ignore bumps, ground changes, and dig requests until `areset`. The checker should therefore drive a splattered Lemming with `ground=1`, `dig=1`, and alternating bumps and confirm that all four outputs remain zero. It should also assert `areset=1` afterward and confirm that the machine returns to `walk_left=1` with the other outputs zero, proving that reset clears the splatter flag and reinitializes the remembered direction.

Another subtle error is allowing a bump on the same cycle that ground disappears to change the remembered walking direction. The spec says that being bumped in the same cycle as ground disappears, but not yet falling, does not affect direction. Similarly, a bump on the cycle ground reappears while still falling must not affect direction. A checker can start walk-left, assert `ground=0` and `bump_right=1` together, then restore ground within the safe fall window and verify that the Lemming resumes walking left. The same test can be repeated at the landing edge with `ground=1` and `bump_right=1` while `aaah` was previously high.

Finally, the checker should test precedence directly. From walk-left on ground, assert `dig=1` and `bump_right=1`; expected outputs are `digging=1`, `walk_left=0`, `walk_right=0`, `aaah=0`. Then assert `ground=0` while digging; expected outputs are `aaah=1`, `digging=0`, and both walking outputs zero. After ground returns within 20 cycles, the Lemming must resume walking left, not right. This catches implementations that let bumping override digging or that forget the remembered direction across a dig-then-fall sequence.
