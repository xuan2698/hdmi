The most useful point for the final checker is to treat the fall counter as part of the state and to evaluate splatter on the transition into the landing state, not on the falling rows themselves. A common mistake is to count the landing row as another fall cycle or to clear the counter too early when a bump or dig request appears. The public spec says the Lemming splatters if it falls for more than 20 clock cycles and then hits the ground. So exactly 20 falling rows followed by a `ground=1` row is safe; 21 falling rows followed by `ground=1` splatters. The landing row itself must not increment the counter, because `ground=1` means the Lemming is no longer falling on that cycle.

A second useful point is that bumps are ignored whenever falling or digging has precedence. In the walking state with `ground=1`, `dig=1` starts digging and suppresses any direction switch from `bump_left` or `bump_right`. In the walking state with `ground=0`, falling starts and suppresses both digging and direction switching. While falling, bumps do not change the remembered direction. While digging, bumps also do not change direction. When both bumps are asserted during an ordinary walking row with `ground=1` and no dig, the direction toggles once, not twice.

The checker should also remember that the outputs are Moore outputs determined by the current state. `walk_left` and `walk_right` are asserted only in the walking state and only for the remembered direction. `aaah` is asserted only in the falling state. `digging` is asserted only in the digging state. In the splattered state all four outputs are zero forever until reset. Reset is asynchronous and positive-edge triggered, returning the machine to walking left.

A short worked example: suppose the machine is walking right, `ground=0`, and `bump_left=1`. Because falling has precedence, the bump is ignored for direction. The machine outputs `aaah=1`, both walking outputs zero, and increments the fall counter. On the next row, if `ground=1`, the machine lands, resumes walking right, and does not increment the counter on that landing row. If the counter had already exceeded 20, the landing row instead produces all-zero outputs and permanent splatter. This example captures the key checker behavior: precedence decides the next state, the counter advances only on true falling cycles, and splatter is decided when the fall ends.
