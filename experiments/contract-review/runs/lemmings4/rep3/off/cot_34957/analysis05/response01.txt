The key distinguishing scenario for the final checker is the interaction between a remembered direction, a fall that is exactly at the splatter threshold, and a bump that arrives on the landing cycle. The public spec says splatter occurs when a Lemming "falls for more than 20 clock cycles then hits the ground." So a fall duration of exactly 20 cycles must land safely and resume walking in the remembered direction; a duration of 21 must splatter. The checker should therefore count fall cycles as the number of sampled edges spent in the falling state, not the number of edges since ground first went low. This matters because the edge where ground disappears begins the fall but is not itself a completed fall cycle, and the edge where ground reappears performs the landing test using the accumulated count.

A second subtle point is that a bump on the landing edge is ignored for direction memory. Suppose the Lemming was walking left, ground vanished, it fell for some safe number of cycles, and on the cycle ground returns `bump_right=1` is also asserted. The spec says being bumped "when the ground reappears while still falling" does not affect walking direction. The post-edge state should therefore be walking left, not walking right. The checker must apply the landing transition before considering bump, and must not let the bump flip the remembered direction on that same edge. The same rule applies on the edge where ground disappears: if `ground=0` and `bump_left=1` coincide while the Lemming was walking right, fall has precedence, so the Lemming begins falling and remembers right; the bump does not switch it to left.

A third point is that digging is a persistent mode, not a one-cycle action. Once a walking Lemming on ground receives `dig=1`, the `digging` output stays high across subsequent edges while ground remains 1, even if `dig` later goes low. The checker should not require `dig=1` to remain asserted. Digging ends only when `ground=0`; at that edge the Lemming transitions to falling, and the remembered walking direction is the direction it had before digging began. Bumps during digging are ignored, and if `dig=1` and a bump coincide while walking on ground, dig wins over the direction change.

Finally, reset must be treated as an asynchronous positive-edge event with highest priority, and the outputs reported for a row must reflect the post-edge state. If `areset=1`, the checker should force walking left and clear fall count, splatter, and digging, regardless of all other inputs. If the machine was splattered and `areset=0`, all four outputs must remain zero forever. A useful compact test is: splattered state, then `areset=1` with `ground=0` and `dig=1`; the row must show `walk_left=1` and the other three outputs zero, proving reset overrides both splatter and the fall/dig precedence.
