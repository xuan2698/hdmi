The counter is a four-digit BCD incrementer with synchronous active-high reset. The state `q` is partitioned into four nibbles: `q[3:0]` ones, `q[7:4]` tens, `q[11:8]` hundreds, `q[15:12]` thousands. Each nibble is expected to hold a valid decimal digit 0–9 in normal operation; the public spec does not require the checker to police invalid BCD encodings, only the counting and enable behavior.

The transition rule is: at a rising edge, if `reset` is high, all of `q` becomes zero. Otherwise the counter increments by one in BCD. The ones digit advances 0→1→…→9→0; when the ones digit was 9 it wraps to 0 and the tens digit increments; when tens was 9 and ones was 9, tens wraps and hundreds increments; and so on through the thousands digit. The thousands digit wraps from 9 to 0 with no further carry, so the full 16-bit state cycles through 0000–9999 and returns to 0000. Because reset is synchronous, a high `reset` sampled at an edge overrides the increment for that edge; a `reset` value that changes after the sampled edge does not affect the already-sampled row.

The enable outputs are purely combinational functions of the settled post-edge state, not of the next state and not of pre-edge values. `ena[0]` is high iff the ones digit equals 9. `ena[1]` is high iff the tens digit equals 9 and the ones digit equals 9. `ena[2]` is high iff hundreds, tens, and ones are all 9. Equivalently, `ena[k]` indicates that digit k+1 will be incremented on the next non-reset rising edge. The checker should compute these from the same `q` it samples for that row, after the posedge has settled.

A key alignment point for the final checker: each row’s inputs are driven before the sampled rising edge, and optional mid-cycle input changes occur after sampling and before the falling edge, carried into the next row. Thus the checker must (1) capture `reset` as of the sampled edge, (2) capture the settled `q` and `ena` after that edge, (3) verify reset precedence and the BCD increment relative to the prior sampled state, and (4) verify `ena` against the current `q` only. Any `reset` change observed mid-cycle must be deferred to the next row’s edge, never applied retroactively.

Worked example: after a rising edge the sampled state is `q = 0x0099` (ones 9, tens 9, hundreds 0, thousands 0). Then `ena[0]` is high (ones = 9), `ena[1]` is high (tens = 9 and ones = 9), and `ena[2]` is low (hundreds ≠ 9). On the next rising edge with `reset` low, the state becomes `0x0100`. If `reset` rises after the `0x0099` sample, that change is carried to the next row and only forces zero at the following rising edge; the `0x0099` row’s `ena` values remain those computed from `0x0099`.

The most useful checker focus is therefore the pairing of (prior sampled state, sampled reset) → (new sampled `q`), plus `ena` recomputed from the new `q`, with strict deferral of mid-cycle input changes. Treat any initial `q` as an unconstrained seed and check only transitions and enables relative to it.
