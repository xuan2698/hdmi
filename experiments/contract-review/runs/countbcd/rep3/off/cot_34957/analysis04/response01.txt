The design is a 16-bit synchronous BCD counter with a single 1-bit active-high reset input and two output buses: `q[15:0]` and `ena[2:0]`. The 16-bit `q` is partitioned into four 4-bit BCD digits: `q[3:0]` is the ones digit, `q[7:4]` the tens, `q[11:8]` the hundreds, and `q[15:12]` the thousands. Each digit is expected to hold a legal BCD value in 0–9; the counter rolls over from 9999 to 0000. The 3-bit `ena` bus is purely combinational and derived from the settled post-edge `q`: `ena[0]` is high iff the ones digit is 9, `ena[1]` is high iff tens and ones are both 9, and `ena[2]` is high iff hundreds, tens, and ones are all 9. These correspond to the carry conditions for incrementing the next higher digit.

Boundary values matter for the checker. The all-9s state `q = 0x9999` is the rollover boundary: on the next non-reset rising edge the state becomes `0x0000`, and all three enables are high in the `0x9999` row. The state `0x0099` exercises the partial-carry case: `ena[0]` and `ena[1]` high, `ena[2]` low, and the next state `0x0100`. States with no 9 in the low digits have all enables low. The reset input is sampled at the rising edge; a high reset at that edge forces `q = 0x0000` regardless of the prior state, and the enables in that row are computed from the resulting `0x0000` (all low). A reset change occurring after the sampled edge is deferred to the next row and must not retroactively alter the current row’s `q` or `ena`.

The key alignment point for the final Python checker is the row model. Each row is defined by inputs driven before the sampled rising edge, a sampled `reset` value, and the settled post-edge `q` and `ena`. The checker should capture `reset` as of the sampled edge, capture settled `q` and `ena` after that edge, verify reset precedence (high reset at the edge yields `q = 0x0000`), verify the BCD increment from the prior sampled state when reset is low, and verify `ena` solely against the current sampled `q`. Mid-cycle input changes observed after sampling must be carried forward to the next row’s edge and never applied to the current row. The initial `q` is an unconstrained seed; only transitions and enables relative to it are checked.

Worked example: suppose the prior sampled state is `q = 0x0098`. On the next rising edge with `reset` low, the new state is `0x0099`. In that row, `ena[0]` is high (ones = 9), `ena[1]` is high (tens = 9 and ones = 9), and `ena[2]` is low (hundreds = 0). If `reset` then rises mid-cycle after this sample, that change is deferred: the `0x0099` row’s enables remain those computed from `0x0099`, and only the following rising edge forces `0x0000`. The checker must therefore pair (prior sampled state, sampled reset) with the new sampled `q`, then recompute `ena` from that new `q`, while strictly deferring any mid-cycle input changes.
