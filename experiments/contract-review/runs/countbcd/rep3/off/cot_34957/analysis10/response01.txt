The public interface is a 16-bit BCD counter with a 1-bit synchronous active-high reset and a 3-bit enable vector. The 16-bit `q` output packs four 4-bit BCD digits: `q[3:0]` is the ones digit, `q[7:4]` the tens, `q[11:8]` the hundreds, and `q[15:12]` the thousands. Each nibble is constrained to the legal BCD range 0–9; values 10–15 are not valid counter states. The 3-bit `ena` output corresponds to the upper three digits: `ena[0]` for the tens digit, `ena[1]` for the hundreds digit, and `ena[2]` for the thousands digit. There is no enable for the ones digit, since it increments on every non-reset edge.

Boundary values matter in two places. First, the counter wraps from `0x9999` to `0x0000` on the next non-reset edge, and the thousands digit rolls over from 9 to 0. Second, the enable conditions are boundary tests on the settled state: `ena[0]` is high exactly when the ones digit is 9, `ena[1]` when tens and ones are both 9, and `ena[2]` when hundreds, tens, and ones are all 9. These are carry-lookahead signals for the next increment, so they are functions of the post-edge settled `q`, not the pre-edge state.

The increment rule is standard ripple-carry BCD: the ones digit always increments modulo 10; each higher digit increments only when all lower digits were 9. Digits that do not receive a carry hold their value. Reset has precedence: when the sampled `reset` is high, the settled outputs must be `q = 0x0000` and `ena = 0`, regardless of the prior state.

The subtle point for the final checker is the timing relationship between `q` and `ena`. Both are sampled after the posedge has settled, and both must be compared against the same settled state. The enables are not registered versions of the pre-edge carry conditions; they are combinational functions of the current `q`. A design that computes enables from the pre-edge state will produce a one-cycle-late enable vector that still has correct `q`, so a checker comparing only `q` would miss the defect.

Worked example: suppose the prior sampled state is `q = 0x0098` with `reset` low. After the edge, the settled state is `q = 0x0099`. The correct enables are `ena[0] = 1` (ones is 9), `ena[1] = 1` (tens and ones are both 9), and `ena[2] = 0` (hundreds is 0). A lagging design would output `ena = 0` derived from `0x0098`, and only an enable check against the captured settled `q` would catch it.

For the checker, sample `reset` at the edge, capture settled `q` and `ena` together, apply reset precedence first, then verify the BCD increment from the prior sampled state, and finally verify `ena` as a pure function of the captured `q`. Mid-cycle input changes belong to the next row and must not be folded into the current comparison. Separating state-update correctness from enable-computation correctness is the key discipline.
