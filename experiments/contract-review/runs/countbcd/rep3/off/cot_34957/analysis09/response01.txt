The counter is a synchronous BCD accumulator with a single active-high reset and a 16-bit output `q` partitioned into four 4-bit decimal digits: `q[3:0]` ones, `q[7:4]` tens, `q[11:8]` hundreds, `q[15:12]` thousands. The three-bit `ena` output is a combinational look-ahead derived from the current settled `q`, not a registered product of the increment. This is the central structural fact the checker must encode.

State transitions occur only on the rising clock edge. If `reset` is sampled high at that edge, the next state is `q = 0x0000` unconditionally, and all enables are low because every digit is zero. If `reset` is sampled low, the ones digit increments modulo 10: `q[3:0]` goes from 0..8 to the next value, and from 9 back to 0. The upper digits increment only when the lower digits are all 9 at the sampled state. Concretely, the tens digit increments iff the ones digit was 9; the hundreds digit increments iff tens and ones were both 9; the thousands digit increments iff hundreds, tens, and ones were all 9. Digits that do not receive a carry hold their value. This is a standard ripple-carry BCD count, so the post-edge value is fully determined by the pre-edge sampled `q` and the sampled `reset`.

The enables are evaluated on the post-edge settled `q`, not on the pre-edge state. `ena[0]` is high iff the ones digit of the settled `q` equals 9. `ena[1]` is high iff the tens and ones digits of the settled `q` are both 9. `ena[2]` is high iff the hundreds, tens, and ones digits of the settled `q` are all 9. Equivalently, each enable is the carry-out of the lower digits computed from the current `q`. Because the enables are look-ahead conditions for the next increment, they must reflect the state that will be incremented on the following edge, which is the state settled after the current edge.

The off-by-one-cycle defect arises when a design computes enables from the pre-edge state rather than the settled post-edge state. In the worked example, prior `q = 0x0098` with `reset` low settles to `q = 0x0099` after the edge. Correct enables are `ena[0] = 1`, `ena[1] = 1`, `ena[2] = 0`, because the settled ones and tens digits are both 9 while the hundreds digit is 0. A lagging design would output all zeros, derived from `0x0098`, and a checker that only compares `q` would pass it. Comparing `ena` against a pure function of the captured settled `q` catches this.

For the final Python checker, the useful discipline is: sample `reset` at the edge, then capture settled `q` and `ena` together after that edge. Apply reset precedence first: if the sampled `reset` is high, require `q = 0x0000` and `ena = 0`. Otherwise, verify the BCD increment from the prior sampled state, then verify `ena` as a pure function of the captured `q`. Mid-cycle input changes belong to the next row and must not be folded into the current comparison. This separation of state-update correctness from enable-computation correctness is the key point; it is what makes the checker sensitive to the lagging-enable bug while remaining robust to stimulus timing.
