The most useful distinguishing point for the final checker is the interaction between reset and the carry-enable outputs. A naive checker that computes `ena` purely from the sampled `q` will be wrong on any reset cycle where the pre-edge count happens to end in one or more 9s. The correct rule is: if the latched `reset` is 1, the next `q` is 0 and all three `ena` bits are 0; otherwise the next `q` is the BCD increment of the latched `q`, and `ena` reflects the carry conditions of the latched `q` (ones digit 9 enables the tens increment, ones and tens both 9 enables the hundreds increment, and so on). Reset must gate `ena` to zero regardless of what the digits look like.

A second point is power-up. The public spec gives no initial value for `q` or `ena` and imposes no behavior before the first reset. A checker must not assume `q=0` at time zero or compare `ena` against a guessed initial state. The safe policy is to treat sampled rows as unknown until a `reset=1` posedge has been observed, then begin checking from the known zero state that reset establishes. Seeding expectations from an assumed zero without an observed reset can produce false failures against a legal design whose flops power up arbitrarily.

A third point is mid-cycle input changes. A `reset` that goes high between edges is carried forward and takes effect only at the next posedge. The checker should latch `reset` and `q` at each posedge, compute the expected next `q` and expected `ena` from those latched values, and only then compare against the sampled outputs. Mid-cycle transitions must not influence the expected values for the current row; they belong to the next row's latched inputs.

Worked example: at a posedge the checker latches `q=16'h0029`, `reset=0`. Ones=9, so the tens digit increments: expected next `q=16'h0030`, and `ena[0]=1` this cycle; `ena[1]` and `ena[2]` are 0 because not all lower digits are 9. If `reset` then rises mid-cycle, that change is ignored until the next posedge, where the expected next state becomes `q=0` with all `ena=0`.

The key point for the final checker: latch `reset` and `q` at each posedge, derive expected next `q` and expected `ena` solely from those latched values (with reset gating `ena` to zero), require an observed reset before trusting any state, and never let mid-cycle transitions or an assumed power-up value affect expectations.
