The key timing distinction is between what the prediction interface observes and what the edge commits. The prediction outputs (`predict_taken`, `predict_history`) are combinational functions of the pre-edge state: the PHT entry selected by `predict_pc ^ ghr` (7-bit XOR, 7-bit index into 128 entries) and the current GHR value. These outputs must reflect the state as it stands before any same-cycle training write takes effect. Training only modifies the PHT at the next positive clock edge, so a prediction and a training that target the same PHT entry in the same cycle do not interfere on the read side — the prediction sees the old counter, and the training's effect appears only after the edge.

The GHR arbitration is the subtle part. The spec says training takes precedence over prediction when both want to modify the GHR, but the precise condition is `train_mispredicted`, not merely `train_valid`. A correct (non-mispredicted) training does not recover the GHR; it only updates the PHT. In that case, if a prediction is also valid, the prediction's shift still applies at the edge. Only a mispredicted training forces GHR recovery to `(train_history << 1) | train_taken`, and in that case the prediction's shift is suppressed regardless of whether the prediction targeted the same PHT entry. Index aliasing between the training and prediction is irrelevant to GHR arbitration — the two concerns are independent.

So the edge behavior decomposes cleanly: (1) if `train_valid`, write the updated saturating counter to the PHT entry indexed by `train_pc ^ train_history`; (2) for the GHR, if `train_mispredicted`, load the recovered value; else if `predict_valid`, shift in `predict_taken`; else hold. The prediction outputs are computed from pre-edge state and are unaffected by the same-cycle training write.

Worked example: GHR=0, PHT entry 0 holds counter 2 (predicting taken). Same cycle: `predict_valid=1, predict_pc=0`; `train_valid=1, train_pc=0, train_taken=0, train_mispredicted=0`. Prediction outputs `predict_taken=1`, `predict_history=0`. At the edge, entry 0 decrements to 1, and since the training is not mispredicted, the prediction shift applies: GHR becomes 1. A checker that suppresses the shift on any `train_valid` would wrongly expect GHR=0.

For the final checker, the useful invariant is: gate the GHR commit solely on `train_mispredicted` (recovery) versus `predict_valid` (shift), and treat PHT writes as unconditional on `train_valid` at the edge. Also honor asynchronous active-high reset immediately, discarding pending same-cycle effects rather than deferring them to the next edge.
