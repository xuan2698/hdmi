The reset and power-up behavior of this gshare predictor is straightforward but worth pinning down precisely, because the checker will sample immediately after the posedge and must not be confused by the interaction between asynchronous reset and same-cycle prediction/training requests.

Reset is asynchronous and active-high. When `areset` is asserted, the predictor is forced into its initial state without waiting for a clock edge: the 7-bit global history register (GHR) is cleared to zero, and the 128-entry pattern history table (PHT) of two-bit saturating counters is initialized. The natural power-up value for each counter is the "weakly not-taken" state (binary `01`, i.e., value 1), which is the conventional gshare initialization and the value the checker should assume unless the spec says otherwise. Because reset is asynchronous, an assertion of `areset` at any point in the cycle — including between edges — takes effect immediately and overrides whatever the combinational prediction logic would otherwise produce.

The important consequence for the checker is that reset dominates same-cycle activity. If `areset` is high while `predict_valid` and/or `train_valid` are also high, the reset wins: the GHR is held at zero, the PHT is held at its initial values, and no training write or prediction-driven shift is committed at the edge. The checker should treat a cycle with `areset=1` as producing the reset state on the outputs (`predict_history=0`, and `predict_taken` reflecting the initial counter value for the indexed entry, i.e., not-taken) and should not expect any pending training or prediction effect to survive into the next cycle. In other words, reset discards pending same-cycle effects rather than deferring them.

Power-up without an explicit reset assertion is the same as the reset state: GHR=0 and all counters at their initial value. The checker can seed its model this way at time zero and then apply the normal edge rules once `areset` is deasserted.

A short worked example clarifies the reset-dominance rule. Suppose the model is running normally with GHR=5 and PHT entry 3 holding counter 3 (strongly taken). In some cycle `areset=1` is asserted while `predict_valid=1, predict_pc=3` and `train_valid=1, train_pc=3, train_taken=0, train_mispredicted=1`. The prediction logic would nominally output `predict_taken=1, predict_history=5`, and the edge would nominally recover the GHR to `(train_history<<1)|0` and decrement entry 3. But because reset is active, the checker must instead expect the outputs to reflect the reset state and, at the edge, GHR=0 with entry 3 restored to its initial counter value. The training and prediction requests are effectively swallowed.

The single most useful point for the final checker: model `areset` as an immediate, overriding assignment to the initial state that preempts both the PHT write and the GHR commit for that cycle, and initialize all 128 counters to the weakly-not-taken value at power-up. Everything else — the `train_mispredicted`-gated GHR recovery versus `predict_valid` shift, and the unconditional-on-`train_valid` PHT write — applies only when `areset` is low.
