One likely implementation mistake is to treat the prediction and training paths as if they share a single “current” GHR value, rather than recognizing that the prediction interface must use the pre-edge GHR while training may override the next-state GHR. In a gshare predictor, `predict_history` is the history register value that was actually XORed with `predict_pc` to form the PHT index for this prediction. It is not the shifted history after incorporating the predicted direction, and it is not `train_history`. A common bug is to compute `predict_history` from the next-state logic, so that when `predict_valid` and `train_valid` coincide, the output reflects the training recovery or the prediction shift instead of the value used for the lookup.

A second, closely related mistake is to let a same-cycle training write affect the combinational PHT read for prediction. The spec explicitly says the prediction sees the PHT state before training, because training only modifies the table at the next positive clock edge. An implementation that performs the PHT update combinationally, or that reads through a bypass path from the training write, will produce the wrong `predict_taken` when prediction and training hit the same index.

A third mistake is mishandling GHR precedence. When training for a misprediction and a prediction occur in the same cycle, training takes precedence for the next GHR state. The prediction’s would-be shift must be suppressed. The recovered GHR should be `shift(train_history, train_taken)`, not `shift(current_GHR, predicted_direction)`.

Short worked example: current GHR = `0000000`, PHT entry 0 = counter 2, and both `predict_pc ^ GHR` and `train_pc ^ train_history` equal 0. With `predict_valid=1`, `train_valid=1`, `train_taken=0`, `train_mispredicted=1`, the outputs must be `predict_history=0000000` and `predict_taken=1`. At the edge, entry 0 saturates 2→1 and the GHR is recovered to `shift(0000000,0)=0000000`; training wins over the prediction shift. A checker should flag any implementation where the training write changes the same-cycle prediction output, where `predict_history` is post-shift, or where the prediction shift overrides misprediction recovery.
