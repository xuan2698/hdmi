One implementation mistake worth targeting in the checker is conflating the two independent effects of a training request: the PHT counter update and the GHR update. The spec ties them together only conditionally. A training request always updates the addressed 2-bit saturating counter at the next positive edge, but it only overrides the GHR when `train_mispredicted=1`. If the checker treats any `train_valid` as "training wins the GHR," it will wrongly suppress a legitimate prediction-driven GHR shift on a correct training. Conversely, if it treats GHR arbitration as depending on PHT index equality, it will miss the case where a mispredicted training and a prediction touch different entries but still compete for the single GHR.

The clean model is: compute the prediction's PHT read and `predict_history` from the pre-edge state; compute the training's PHT write target from the pre-edge state; at the edge, apply the PHT write unconditionally when `train_valid`, and choose the next GHR as follows. If `train_mispredicted`, recover to `(train_history << 1) | train_taken` (with the 7-bit truncation), regardless of whether the prediction was valid or which PHT entry it used. Otherwise, if `predict_valid`, shift in the predicted direction: `(ghr << 1) | predict_taken`. Otherwise hold. This makes the arbitration depend on `train_mispredicted`, not on `train_valid` alone and not on index aliasing.

A short worked example: suppose GHR=0, entry 0 holds counter 2, and in one cycle `predict_valid=1, predict_pc=0` while `train_valid=1, train_pc=0, train_taken=0, train_mispredicted=0`. The prediction must output `predict_taken=1` and `predict_history=0` from the old counter. At the edge, entry 0 decrements to 1, and because the training is not mispredicted, the prediction's shift applies, so GHR becomes 1. A checker that lets any training suppress the shift would incorrectly expect GHR=0.

The same structure also handles the mispredicted case: with `train_mispredicted=1`, the prediction still reports the old counter value and old history, but the edge applies the training recovery and suppresses the prediction shift. The checker should therefore separate "what the prediction interface observes" from "what the edge commits," and gate only the GHR commit on `train_mispredicted`. It should also apply asynchronous reset immediately, discarding pending same-cycle training and prediction effects rather than waiting for the next edge.
