The timing contract centers on a strict separation between combinational outputs and edge-updated state. All outputs (`predict_taken`, `predict_history`) must be functions of the pre-edge state: the current 7-bit GHR and the current 128-entry PHT of 2-bit saturating counters. Inputs sampled at a posedge (`predict_valid`, `predict_pc`, `train_valid`, `train_taken`, `train_mispredicted`, `train_history`, `train_pc`) drive the next-state computation, but they must not leak into the outputs of the same cycle. In particular, when a prediction and a training request target the same PHT index in the same cycle, the prediction must report the counter value as it stood before the edge; the training write becomes visible only after the edge. Likewise `predict_history` must be the GHR value used to form the prediction index, not a value already shifted by the predicted branch.

The prediction index is `predict_pc ^ ghr` (both 7 bits), selecting one of 128 counters. `predict_taken` is the MSB of that counter (or the standard taken encoding), and `predict_history` is the unmodified GHR. These are pure combinational reads of state.

At the edge, priority is fixed. Asynchronous active-high reset dominates everything: it clears the GHR and PHT regardless of other inputs. Absent reset, if `train_valid=1` and `train_mispredicted=1`, the GHR is recovered to the state immediately after the mispredicting branch, computed from `train_history` and `train_taken` (shift in the actual outcome). This recovery outranks any same-cycle prediction shift, because the predicted instruction is younger and will be flushed. If training is valid but not mispredicted, no GHR recovery occurs, and a same-cycle prediction may still shift the GHR. If neither recovery nor prediction applies, the GHR holds. The PHT write, when `train_valid=1`, updates the counter at `train_pc ^ train_history` toward `train_taken` (saturating), and this write is independent of the GHR priority chain.

A short worked example: GHR=127, `predict_valid=1`, `predict_pc=0` gives index 127; with PHT[127]=3 the outputs are `predict_taken=1`, `predict_history=127`. Same cycle: `train_valid=1`, `train_pc=0`, `train_history=127`, `train_taken=0`, `train_mispredicted=1`. Training index is also 127. The prediction still reports taken with history 127 because the write is post-edge. At the edge, PHT[127] decrements to 2, and the GHR becomes `(127<<1)|0 = 126`, since mispredicted-training recovery beats the prediction shift. A buggy design that shifts first would yield 127; one that applies the training write before reading would report not-taken.

For the final Python checker, model only the GHR and 128 counters as state. Compute outputs from pre-edge state, then apply reset, then the PHT write if `train_valid`, then the GHR priority chain: reset, mispredicted-training recovery, prediction shift, hold. This ordering directly exposes the common errors of combinationally leaking edge updates and mishandling GHR priority.
