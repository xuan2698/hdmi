The public interface is narrow and fully combinational on the read side, with all state updates occurring at the positive clock edge. Inputs are `areset`, `predict_valid`, `predict_pc[6:0]`, `train_valid`, `train_taken`, `train_mispredicted`, `train_history[6:0]`, and `train_pc[6:0]`; outputs are `predict_taken` and `predict_history[6:0]`. The 7-bit widths on `predict_pc`, `train_pc`, `train_history`, and `predict_history` are exact: the full address space is 0–127, and the GHR likewise spans 0–127. The index is formed by XORing the low 7 bits of the PC with the 7-bit GHR, yielding a 7-bit value in 0–127 that selects one of 128 two-bit saturating counters. Because both operands are exactly 7 bits, the XOR is closed over the index space; there is no truncation or extension to reason about, and every PC/history pair maps to exactly one PHT entry.

Boundary values matter for the checker. `predict_pc = 0` with `train_history = 0` yields index 0; `predict_pc = 127` with `train_history = 127` yields index 0 as well (127 XOR 127 = 0), which is a useful aliasing probe. `predict_pc = 127`, `train_history = 0` gives index 127. The two-bit counters saturate at 0 and 3; training `taken=1` increments toward 3, `taken=0` decrements toward 0, and the prediction is the counter's MSB (or equivalently counter ≥ 2). The checker should drive counters to known states via training rather than assume a reset encoding, since the spec only guarantees a defined reset state.

Timing semantics: `predict_taken` and `predict_history` are combinational functions of the current PHT entry and current GHR, sampled after the posedge has settled. A `predict_valid` request updates the GHR at the next edge by shifting in the predicted direction. A `train_valid` request updates the PHT at the next edge; if `train_mispredicted`, the GHR is recovered to the state immediately after the mispredicting branch, derived from `train_history`. When prediction and mispredicted training collide in the same cycle, training wins for the GHR — the predicted branch is discarded. For the PHT, a same-cycle predict and train of the same index do not conflict observably: the prediction output reflects the pre-edge counter, and the training write lands at the edge.

Reset is asynchronous and active-high and dominates all state. While `areset` is high, neither the prediction shift nor the training recovery is observable. After reset, the GHR reads its reset value and the PHT is in some defined state. The checker should probe reset-dominance by asserting `areset` concurrently with a mispredicted training carrying a distinctive `train_history`, then releasing reset and confirming the GHR shows the reset value rather than `train_history` or a shifted history. It should also confirm that a same-cycle predict+train on the same index returns the pre-edge counter, and that a same-cycle predict+mispredicted-train yields the training-derived GHR, not the prediction-shifted one.
