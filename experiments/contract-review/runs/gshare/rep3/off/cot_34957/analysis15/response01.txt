The design is a gshare predictor with two stateful structures: a 7-bit global history register (GHR) and a 128-entry PHT of 2-bit saturating counters. The index into the PHT is `predict_pc ^ GHR` (7 bits each, XOR into 7 bits). The prediction interface is combinational in the sense that `predict_taken` and `predict_history` reflect the current PHT state and current GHR for the supplied `predict_pc`, sampled at the clock edge; the GHR update for a predicted branch happens at the next posedge. The training interface updates the PHT counter for the trained branch's index (`train_pc ^ train_history`) at the next posedge, and, when `train_mispredicted` is asserted, recovers the GHR to the state immediately after the mispredicting branch — i.e., the GHR is set to `{train_history[5:0], train_taken}` (shift in the actual outcome), not merely to `train_history`.

Arbitration: when `train_valid` and `predict_valid` both occur in the same cycle, training wins for the GHR — the predicted branch is discarded, so the GHR takes the training-derived value (recovery shift if mispredicted, otherwise unchanged from the training path's perspective). For the PHT, a same-cycle prediction and training of the same index do not conflict observably: the prediction output reflects the pre-edge counter, and the training write lands at the edge, so the prediction sees the old state. This is the timing-diagram behavior the spec calls out.

Reset is asynchronous and active-high, and it dominates everything: while `areset` is high, neither the prediction shift nor the training recovery is observable; after reset the GHR reads its reset value (spec pins width, not necessarily the exact reset encoding beyond "defined"), and the PHT is in some defined reset state. The checker should not assume a specific counter reset encoding unless a later pass pins it; instead it should establish counter state via training before probing behavior, and it should probe reset-dominance by asserting `areset` concurrently with a mispredicted training carrying a distinctive `train_history`, then releasing reset and confirming the GHR shows the reset value rather than `train_history` or a shifted history.

Worked example: with GHR=`H0` and PHT index `I = P ^ H0` trained to a known counter, drive one cycle with `areset=1`, `predict_valid=1`, `predict_pc=P`, `train_valid=1`, `train_mispredicted=1`, `train_history=H1` (`H1 != H0`). After deasserting reset and settling the next posedge, `predict_history` must equal the GHR reset value, and the PHT entry at `I` must reflect reset state, not a training update from `H1`. Seeing `H1` or a shifted `H0` indicates a reset-arbitration bug.

Key point for the final checker: model reset as an asynchronous, dominating clear of both GHR and PHT; sample only after deassertion plus a settled edge; never depend on unspecified power-up counter values — drive counters to known states through training, and treat post-reset PHT contents as "some defined state" unless a later pass fixes the encoding.
