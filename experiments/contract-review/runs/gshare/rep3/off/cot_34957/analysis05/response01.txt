**Distinguishing input example (prose).** The most useful short scenario for the final checker is one that separates three otherwise-easy-to-conflate behaviors: (a) prediction is combinational from pre-edge state, (b) training indexes the PHT with `train_history` rather than the live history register, and (c) a mispredicted training recovers the history register to `{train_history[5:0], train_taken}` and takes precedence over a same-cycle prediction's history update. A single cycle can exercise all three if the live history register, the `train_history` input, and the `predict_pc`/`train_pc` are chosen so that the two XOR indices differ and the recovered history differs from both the current history and the prediction-shifted history.

Concretely, suppose after reset the live history register is 0. Drive `predict_valid=1` with `predict_pc=7'b0000001` (index 1) and, in the same cycle, `train_valid=1`, `train_mispredicted=1`, `train_taken=1`, `train_history=7'b1000000` (64), `train_pc=7'b1000000` (index 64). The prediction must read PHT[1] and output `predict_history=0` (the pre-edge register), regardless of the training. The training must write PHT[64] (not PHT[0] or PHT[1]), using `train_taken` to increment that counter, and at the edge must set the history register to `{train_history[5:0], train_taken} = {6'b000000,1} = 7'b0000001` (1), not to the prediction-shift result `{0, predicted_taken}` and not to a shift of the live 0. On the next cycle, with no training, a prediction at `predict_pc=7'b1000001` (index 65) should see `predict_history=1` and read PHT[65], confirming the recovery landed in the register and that the earlier write went to index 64.

This example is discriminating because a checker that mistakenly indexes training by the live history (0) would write PHT[0] instead of PHT[64]; one that lets prediction win the history update would leave history at 0 or 1 depending on the predicted bit but from the wrong source; one that recovers history by shifting the live register rather than `train_history` would produce a different value; and one that makes prediction see the same-cycle PHT write would report a different `predict_taken` if PHT[1] and PHT[64] start in different states. Choosing initial PHT states so PHT[1] and PHT[64] differ (e.g., preload via earlier training) makes the combinational-read check observable in `predict_taken` as well as in `predict_history`.

**Checker focus.** The final Python checker should sample `predict_taken` and `predict_history` as combinational functions of pre-edge state, verify the PHT write address equals `train_pc ^ train_history` (7-bit), verify the recovered history equals `{train_history[5:0], train_taken}` on mispredicted training, and confirm training overrides prediction for the history register when both are valid. Counter saturation at 0 and 3, and the MSB-as-direction rule at the 1↔2 boundary, are the remaining high-value assertions.
