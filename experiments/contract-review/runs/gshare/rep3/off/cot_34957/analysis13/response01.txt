The key timing distinction is between values sampled at the positive edge and values that are only visible after that edge. For this gshare predictor, the prediction outputs are combinational functions of the current state: `predict_taken` comes from the PHT entry selected by `predict_pc ^ history`, and `predict_history` is simply the current 7-bit history register. Both must be stable before the sampled edge and must reflect the pre-edge state. The state updates—PHT counter increment/decrement and history-register shift or recovery—occur at the next positive edge. A checker should therefore drive `predict_valid` and `train_valid` before the edge, sample outputs after the edge has settled, and then inspect the next-cycle state to confirm the edge-triggered updates.

The most important probe is the simultaneous prediction/training case. When `predict_valid=1` and `train_valid=1` with `train_mispredicted=1` occur in the same cycle, the prediction must still use the old PHT counter and old history register. The training request must not combinationally alter either output. At the next edge, training wins arbitration: the history register becomes `train_history`, not the shifted prediction history. The PHT entry selected by `train_pc ^ train_history` is updated according to `train_taken`, but that update is visible only after the edge. If the trained index equals the predicted index, the same-cycle `predict_taken` must still reflect the pre-training counter. This is the cleanest way to catch an implementation that writes the PHT combinationally or that lets the training path override the prediction path in the same cycle.

A second probe is the non-mispredicted training case. With `train_valid=1` and `train_mispredicted=0`, the PHT entry is updated at the next edge, but the history register must remain unchanged. The checker should confirm that `predict_history` in the following cycle still equals the prior history value, and that no prediction-driven shift occurred unless `predict_valid` was also asserted. If `predict_valid=1` and `train_valid=1` with `train_mispredicted=0` occur together, the prediction shift should occur at the next edge, because training does not request history recovery. This distinguishes the two training modes.

A third probe concerns reset. Reset is asynchronous active-high. The checker should assert `areset` while also driving `predict_valid` or `train_valid`, then release reset and verify that the post-reset history register and PHT state are the specified reset values, not values influenced by the simultaneous requests. Because reset is asynchronous, the checker should allow the reset assertion to take effect without waiting for a clock edge, but should sample the post-reset state only after reset is deasserted and the next edge has settled.

Finally, the checker should verify the index hash directly. For a prediction with `predict_pc=P` and pre-edge history `H`, the selected PHT entry is `P ^ H`. The output `predict_history` must equal `H`, not `H` shifted by the predicted direction. This is easy to get wrong when the history register is updated in the same always block that drives the output. The checker can use a directed test where `P ^ H` selects a known counter value and confirm both `predict_taken` and `predict_history` before the edge, then confirm the shifted history after the edge.
