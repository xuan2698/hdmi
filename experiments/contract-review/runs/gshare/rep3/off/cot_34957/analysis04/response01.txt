The interface is small but has several boundary-sensitive details worth pinning down for the checker.

**Port widths and roles.** `areset` is a single asynchronous active-high reset. `predict_valid` and `train_valid` are single-bit enables. `predict_pc` and `train_pc` are each 7 bits, so their legal range is 0–127. `train_history` and `predict_history` are 7-bit global-history values, also 0–127. `train_taken` and `train_mispredicted` are single-bit flags. `predict_taken` is a single-bit output. The PHT has 128 entries of 2-bit saturating counters, indexed by `pc XOR history` truncated to 7 bits; since both operands are 7 bits, the XOR is naturally 7 bits and always in range 0–127. No index masking beyond the 7-bit width is needed.

**Boundary values.** The extreme PCs are 0 and 127; the extreme histories are 0 and 127. The XOR index is therefore also always 0–127, so the checker never needs to reject an out-of-range PHT index. Counter states are 0,1,2,3; saturation means increment stops at 3 and decrement stops at 0. The predicted direction is the counter's MSB (states 2,3 predict taken; 0,1 predict not-taken), so the boundary cases are counter 1→2 (not-taken to taken prediction) and 2→1 (taken to not-taken). History shift boundaries are 0 and 127: shifting left and inserting a bit maps 127 to either 126 or 127 depending on the inserted bit, and maps 0 to 0 or 1.

**Timing boundaries.** All state changes occur at the posedge. Combinational outputs (`predict_taken`, `predict_history`) reflect pre-edge state. A same-cycle PHT write is not visible to that cycle's prediction. Training overrides prediction for the history register when both are valid. Reset is asynchronous, so the checker should treat assertion of `areset` as immediately forcing history and PHT to their reset values, independent of clock edges.

**Worked example.** After reset, history=0. With `predict_valid=1, predict_pc=0`, the index is 0, `predict_history=0`, and `predict_taken` is the MSB of PHT[0] (reset value, typically 0 or 1 per reference). If simultaneously `train_valid=1, train_mispredicted=1, train_history=0, train_taken=1`, the PHT[0] write and history recovery to `{6'b0,1}` occur at the edge, while the prediction output still shows the pre-edge counter and `predict_history=0`.

**Useful checker focus.** The highest-value checks are: (1) prediction outputs are purely combinational from pre-edge state; (2) training uses `train_history`, not the current history register, for indexing; (3) mispredicted training recovers history via `{train_history[5:0], train_taken}`; (4) training wins over prediction for history; (5) counter saturation at 0 and 3. These cover the spec's subtle points without over-constraining implementation details.
