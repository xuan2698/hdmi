The state consists of two elements: a 7-bit global history register and a 128-entry PHT of 2-bit saturating counters. The history register is directly observable through `predict_history`, so its post-reset value is fully constrained: it must be 0. The PHT contents after reset are less tightly pinned by the public spec—the spec does not mandate a particular initial counter value—so the checker should treat the reset-initialized PHT as a model-defined constant (commonly all zeros, giving not-taken predictions) rather than hard-coding a specific first prediction. The key point for the checker is that `predict_taken` after reset must be derived from the model's own initialized PHT entry indexed by `predict_pc ^ 0`, not assumed.

The prediction path is purely combinational with respect to current state: `predict_history` equals the current history register, and `predict_taken` equals the MSB of the PHT entry at index `predict_pc ^ history`. When `predict_valid=1`, the history register is scheduled to update at the next posedge by shifting in the predicted direction (the MSB of the read counter). This update is a next-state effect, not a same-cycle output change—so a prediction request in cycle N does not alter `predict_history` until after the edge ending cycle N.

The training path has two effects. First, it updates the PHT entry at `train_pc ^ train_history` according to `train_taken` using saturating 2-bit counter semantics; this write lands at the next posedge, so a same-cycle prediction of the same entry observes the pre-training value. Second, if `train_mispredicted=1`, the history register is recovered to `train_history` (the state immediately after the mispredicting branch), again at the next posedge.

The critical arbitration rule: when training (specifically a misprediction recovery) and a prediction both want to modify the history register in the same cycle, training wins. The predicted branch is younger and will be flushed, so its history update is discarded. The checker must model this priority explicitly: next_history = train_mispredicted ? train_history : (predict_valid ? shift_in_prediction : current_history), with reset overriding everything to 0.

Reset is asynchronous and active-high, acting as an immediate override independent of the clock edge. While `areset` is high, the history register is forced to 0 and the PHT is forced to its defined initialization; no prediction or training side effects may occur. A `predict_valid` or `train_valid` asserted concurrently with `areset` must not perturb the post-reset state. Because reset is asynchronous, the checker should sample outputs after the posedge has settled and treat the pre-first-reset interval as unconstrained—checking begins only after a reset has been observed.

Worked example: suppose history=0b0000101, `predict_pc`=0b0000000, so index=0b0000101. If that PHT entry holds 0b10 (MSB=1), `predict_taken=1` and `predict_history=0b0000101` in the same cycle. If simultaneously `train_valid=1, train_mispredicted=1, train_history=0b1100000`, then at the next edge history becomes 0b1100000 (training wins over the prediction's shift), and the PHT write targets `train_pc ^ train_history`, not the predicted index. The checker should verify both the priority and that the prediction output in that cycle still reflected the pre-edge state.
