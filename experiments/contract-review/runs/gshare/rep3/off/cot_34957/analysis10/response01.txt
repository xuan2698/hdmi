The public interface is small but the boundary behavior is where a checker earns its keep. All data ports are 7 bits: `predict_pc`, `train_pc`, `train_history`, and the output `predict_history`. The control ports are 1 bit: `areset`, `predict_valid`, `train_valid`, `train_taken`, `train_mispredicted`, and the output `predict_taken`. The PHT index is formed as `predict_pc ^ history` for prediction and `train_pc ^ train_history` for training; both operands are 7 bits, so the XOR is 7 bits and indexes exactly 128 entries. There is no truncation or extension anywhere in the datapath, which means every 7-bit value is a legal address and every 7-bit history is a legal register state. Boundary values therefore matter mainly for counter saturation and for the all-zeros/all-ones patterns that stress XOR aliasing.

The 2-bit saturating counters are the only place where numeric boundaries appear. A counter at `00` must not decrement below `00` on a not-taken training event, and a counter at `11` must not increment above `11` on a taken event. The prediction bit is the counter MSB, so `10` and `11` predict taken while `00` and `01` predict not-taken. The checker should exercise both saturation rails and confirm that repeated training in one direction is idempotent at the rail. The initial PHT contents are part of the public behavior; the checker should not assume a particular reset value unless the spec fixes it, but it must confirm that after reset the table is in the defined initialization and that no stale training survives.

The history register is 7 bits and shifts in the predicted direction on a prediction. The exact shift direction and which bit is inserted are part of the spec's definition of the gshare update; the checker should verify that `predict_history` is the pre-edge register value used to form the index, not the post-edge value. That distinction is the most common source of checker false positives: the output must be the history that was XORed with `predict_pc` in the same cycle.

The arbitration rule is the highest-value target. When `train_mispredicted=1` and `predict_valid=1` coincide, the next history is `train_history`, and the prediction's shift is discarded. When `train_mispredicted=0` but `train_valid=1`, training updates only the PHT and does not touch history. A same-cycle prediction of the same PHT entry must observe the pre-training counter value, because the write lands at the next posedge. The checker should construct a case where the predicted index equals the trained index and confirm the output reflects the old counter while the next cycle reflects the new one.

Reset is asynchronous and active-high, so it overrides the clock. Any `predict_valid` or `train_valid` asserted while `areset` is high must not perturb the post-reset state. The checker should begin meaningful comparison only after observing a reset, treating earlier cycles as unconstrained. A short worked example: with history `0000101` and `predict_pc` `0000000`, the index is `0000101`; if that entry is `10`, `predict_taken=1` and `predict_history=0000101`. If `train_mispredicted=1` with `train_history=1100000` in the same cycle, the next history is `1100000`, and the PHT write targets `train_pc ^ 1100000`, not the predicted index.
