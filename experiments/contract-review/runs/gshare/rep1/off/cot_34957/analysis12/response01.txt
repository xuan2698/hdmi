One implementation mistake worth targeting in the final checker is treating the prediction interface as if it observes post-edge state, or as if a same-cycle training request can influence the prediction output. The spec is explicit that prediction is combinational with respect to the current PHT and GHR, and that training only modifies the PHT at the next positive clock edge. A common buggy RTL pattern is to compute `predict_taken` from a registered copy of the counter, or to let a same-cycle `train_valid` write feed back into the prediction mux. Such a design can pass isolated predict-only and train-only tests, because in those cases the pre-edge and post-edge views coincide or the training effect is not observed until later. It fails only when predict and train collide on the same index in the same cycle.

The checker should therefore construct a case where the PHT entry is in a known state, then in one cycle assert both `predict_valid` and `train_valid` for PCs that hash to the same index, with `train_taken` chosen to flip the counter's MSB. The expected `predict_taken` is the MSB of the counter as it stood before the edge; the expected post-edge counter is the saturating update. If the checker samples `predict_taken` after the edge, or if the DUT lets the training write leak into the prediction path, the observed value will be the new MSB and the test will incorrectly pass a broken design or incorrectly fail a correct one depending on which side the checker models.

A related trap is the GHR update priority. When `train_mispredicted` is asserted, the GHR must be recovered to `((train_history << 1) | train_taken) & 0x7F`, and this must override any prediction-driven shift in the same cycle. A checker that only tests misprediction recovery in cycles with `predict_valid=0` will not catch a design where the prediction shift wins. The high-value assertion is: in a cycle with both `predict_valid` and a mispredicted `train_valid`, `predict_history` reflects the pre-edge GHR, and the next-cycle GHR equals the recovered value, not the shifted prediction value.

Finally, asynchronous active-high reset must dominate. The checker should drive `areset` mid-cycle and confirm that outputs go to zero immediately and that no pending train or predict in that row mutates state. This catches synchronous-reset implementations and designs that still commit a training write on the reset edge. These three checks—pre-edge prediction read, recovery-over-shift priority, and reset dominance—are the most discriminating for this spec.
