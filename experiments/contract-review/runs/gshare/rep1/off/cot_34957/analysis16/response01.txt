The public interface is narrow but the boundary behavior is where a checker earns its keep. Inputs: `areset` (1), `predict_valid` (1), `predict_pc` (7), `train_valid` (1), `train_taken` (1), `train_mispredicted` (1), `train_history` (7), `train_pc` (7). Outputs: `predict_taken` (1), `predict_history` (7). Clock `clk`; sample after posedge settles; inputs are driven before the sampled edge, with optional mid-cycle changes carried into the next row.

Widths matter because the index is 7 bits: `index = predict_pc ^ ghr` (xor of two 7-bit values), giving 0..127 into a 128-entry PHT of 2-bit saturating counters. `train_pc` and `train_history` are also 7 bits, so the training index is `train_pc ^ train_history` — note it uses the *supplied* `train_history`, not the live GHR. This is the single most important boundary: the training address is fully determined by the training inputs, independent of current GHR state. A checker that hashes `train_pc` with the live GHR will diverge on any cycle where `train_history != ghr`.

Boundary values to exercise: pc/history all-zeros (index 0), all-ones (index 0 again, since 0x7F^0x7F=0), and pairs that xor to 0 or 127. Counter saturation: 2-bit counters clamp at `00` and `11`; a taken train on `11` stays `11`, a not-taken on `00` stays `00`. `train_taken` is 1 bit, so only 0/1. `train_mispredicted` gates GHR recovery only; it does not change PHT update direction (that is `train_taken`).

GHR update at the edge has three potential writers with priority reset > misprediction-recovery > prediction-shift. Prediction shift (when `predict_valid`) is `{ghr[5:0], predict_taken}` using the *pre-edge* predicted direction. Recovery (when `train_valid & train_mispredicted`) is `{train_history[5:0], train_taken}`. Training without mispredict does not write GHR, so a coincident predict still shifts. Reset asynchronously clears GHR and all PHT entries to `00`, and outputs must reflect cleared state immediately; a concurrent train must not leak past reset.

Outputs are combinational on pre-edge state: `predict_taken` is the PHT bit selected by the pre-edge index (MSB of the 2-bit counter), and `predict_history` is the pre-edge GHR. Same-cycle training does not affect the read; the write lands at the edge.

Worked example: GHR=`1010101`, PHT[0]=`11`. Cycle N: predict hashes to entry 0 → `predict_taken=1`, `predict_history=1010101`. Same cycle train_mispredicted=1, train_taken=0, train_history=`0000000`, train_pc hashing to 0. At edge: PHT[0]→`10`; GHR→`0000000` (recovery beats shift). Next cycle predict on entry 0 reads `10` → not-taken, history `0000000`.

Checker focus: verify (1) outputs are pre-edge and training-independent; (2) recovery-over-shift priority; (3) reset dominance with post-reset GHR=0 and PHT all-zero; (4) training index uses `train_history`, not live GHR.
