**State and transition analysis**

The design has two pieces of architectural state: a 7-bit global branch history register (`ghr`) and a 128-entry PHT of 2-bit saturating counters. Reset is asynchronous and active-high; it forces `ghr=0` and all PHT entries to their reset value (the spec's worked example uses all-zero, i.e. strongly-not-taken). Reset has absolute priority over every other input, including `predict_valid` and `train_valid`, and it also suppresses any PHT write that would otherwise occur on the same edge. Because reset is asynchronous, an `areset` assertion arriving mid-cycle must be treated as an immediate override by the checker, not deferred to the next sampled edge; once `areset` is low again, normal edge-sampled behavior resumes.

**Prediction path.** When `predict_valid=1` (and reset is low), the index is `predict_pc ^ ghr` (7-bit XOR). The PHT entry at that index is read combinationally to produce `predict_taken` (the counter's MSB, i.e. taken when the counter is in state 2 or 3). The current `ghr` is also emitted as `predict_history`. Crucially, the prediction observes the PHT and `ghr` as they exist *before* the current edge — a training request in the same cycle does not affect the prediction's view, because PHT writes and history updates commit only at the next posedge. At that posedge, the predicted direction is shifted into `ghr` (the predicted branch is assumed to have executed as predicted), unless a higher-priority event overrides.

**Training path.** When `train_valid=1`, the PHT entry at `train_pc ^ train_history` is updated toward `train_taken` using the standard 2-bit saturating counter rule (increment on taken unless already 3, decrement on not-taken unless already 0). This write commits at the posedge. If `train_mispredicted=1`, the history register must additionally be *recovered* to the state immediately after the mispredicting branch — that is, `ghr` is set to `{train_history[5:0], train_taken}` (shift the actual outcome into the supplied history), reflecting the correct post-branch history rather than the speculative one.

**Priority ordering at the posedge.** The next-state function must be ordered: (1) `areset` — clobber everything, no PHT write, no history change; (2) mispredicted training — perform the PHT update *and* recover `ghr` to `{train_history[5:0], train_taken}`; (3) prediction — shift the predicted direction into `ghr`; (4) otherwise hold. Note that a non-mispredicted training still performs its PHT write but does not touch `ghr`. When training (mispredicted) and a prediction for a younger instruction coincide, training wins for the history register because the predicted instruction will be flushed; the PHT write from training still commits, and the prediction's read used the pre-edge PHT value.

**Worked example.** After reset, drive `predict_valid=1`, `predict_pc=P`, `train_valid=0`. Pre-edge state: `ghr=0`, PHT all zero. Outputs: `predict_taken=0`, `predict_history=0`, index `P^0=P`. At the posedge, `ghr` shifts in not-taken (stays 0); no PHT write. If `areset` were still high at that edge, the prediction is ignored and state holds at reset.

**Checker focus.** Implement reset as an immediate override (including mid-cycle), then a single next-state assignment with priority reset > mispredicted-training recovery > prediction shift, and ensure PHT writes are suppressed under reset while prediction reads always use pre-edge state.
