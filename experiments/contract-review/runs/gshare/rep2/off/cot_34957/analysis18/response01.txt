One likely implementation mistake is treating the training update as visible to a same-cycle prediction. The spec says a prediction request samples the PHT state *before* training modifies it; training only changes the counter at the next posedge. A model that computes the trained counter combinationally and then feeds it to `predict_taken` will be wrong whenever prediction and training hit the same index. Similarly, `predict_history` must be the GHR value before the edge, not a value already shifted by the predicted branch or overwritten by training recovery.

Worked example: GHR = `0b0000001`, PHT[index] = `2'b10`. Drive `predict_valid=1` with `predict_pc` matching the training index, and `train_valid=1`, `train_taken=0`, `train_mispredicted=1`, `train_history=0b0000001`, `train_pc` chosen so `train_pc ^ train_history` equals `predict_pc ^ GHR`. At the sampled posedge, `predict_taken` must still be 1 and `predict_history` must be `0b0000001`. After the edge, the counter decrements and the GHR becomes `{train_history[5:0], train_taken}` = `0b0000010`. A combinational-train-first model would output `predict_taken=0`; a prediction-shift-wins model would leave the wrong GHR. The checker should specifically probe same-index predict/train collisions with nonzero history.
