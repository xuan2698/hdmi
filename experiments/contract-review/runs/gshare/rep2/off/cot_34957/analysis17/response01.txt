The key distinguishing behavior is the interaction between the two interfaces in a single cycle, especially when training and prediction collide. A useful short input to pin down is: hold `areset=0`, set `predict_valid=1` with some `predict_pc`, and simultaneously set `train_valid=1`, `train_mispredicted=1`, `train_taken=1`, with a `train_history` and `train_pc` that hash to the *same* PHT entry as the prediction. The checker should verify three things at once:

1. **Prediction output is read-before-write.** In the cycle where the request is sampled, `predict_taken` must reflect the counter value *before* the training update, and `predict_history` must reflect the GHR *before* the edge. If the model applies the training update to the PHT or GHR combinationally and then reads, it will produce the wrong `predict_taken` (or the wrong `predict_history`).

2. **Training wins the GHR write.** After the posedge, the GHR must equal `{train_history[5:0], train_taken}`, not the speculative shift from the prediction. A model that lets the prediction's shift override recovery fails.

3. **Same-index collision is legal and must not corrupt state.** Because index = `pc ^ history`, choosing `train_pc ^ train_history == predict_pc ^ GHR` is easy: pick `train_pc = predict_pc`, `train_history = GHR`. Then both requests touch the same counter. The prediction sees the old counter; the training updates it at the edge.

Worked example (short): start with GHR = `0b0000000`, PHT entry 0 = `2'b10` (weakly taken). Drive `predict_valid=1`, `predict_pc=0` (index 0), and `train_valid=1`, `train_mispredicted=1`, `train_taken=0`, `train_history=0`, `train_pc=0` (also index 0). Sample at the posedge: `predict_taken` must be 1 (old counter 2), `predict_history` must be 0 (old GHR). After the edge: GHR = `{0[5:0],0} = 0`, and PHT[0] decrements to `2'b01`. A model that trains first would output `predict_taken=0`; a model that lets prediction shift the GHR would leave GHR = `0` here (coincidentally same, so pick a nonzero GHR to expose it, e.g. GHR=`0b0000001`, `train_history=0b0000001`, `train_taken=1` → post-edge GHR must be `0b0000011`, not the prediction's `0b0000010`).

Also worth a cheap invariant: with `train_history=7'b1111111` and `train_taken=1`, recovery yields `7'b1111111` again — a fixed point. And drive repeated same-index trainings to confirm the 2-bit counter clamps at 3 (taken) and 0 (not-taken) rather than wrapping. Finally, assert `areset` off-edge with `predict_valid=1` and confirm `predict_history=0` and `predict_taken=0` in that same cycle — a posedge-only reset model will show stale values and should be flagged.
