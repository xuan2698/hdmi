The key distinguishing behavior is the interaction between a same-cycle prediction and a mispredicted training request, especially when they target the same PHT entry. The prediction must be computed from the pre-edge PHT and pre-edge GHR, while the training request updates the PHT at the edge and, if mispredicted, overrides the GHR with `{train_history[5:0], train_taken}`. A non-mispredicted training does not shift the GHR; only a prediction (when no mispredicted training occurs) shifts it with the predicted direction.

A useful short example: suppose the GHR is 0, PHT[0] holds counter 2 (predict taken), and in one cycle `predict_valid=1` with `predict_pc=0` while `train_valid=1`, `train_pc=0`, `train_history=0`, `train_taken=0`, `train_mispredicted=1`. The prediction output must be `predict_taken=1` and `predict_history=0`, because the prediction sees the pre-edge counter 2 and pre-edge GHR 0. At the edge, training decrements PHT[0] to 1 and, because mispredicted, sets GHR to `{000000,0}=0`. The next prediction for pc 0 with GHR 0 would then see counter 1 and predict not-taken. If instead `train_mispredicted=0`, the PHT would still decrement to 1, but the GHR would shift only due to the prediction, becoming `{000000,1}=1`; the prediction output would still be taken with history 0. This distinguishes correct pre-edge prediction semantics, misprediction GHR recovery, and the rule that non-mispredicted training alone does not shift the GHR.
