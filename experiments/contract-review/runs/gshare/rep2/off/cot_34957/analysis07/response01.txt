The key timing distinction for the final checker is between values that are *sampled* at the posedge and values that are *computed* for the next state. The prediction outputs `predict_taken` and `predict_history` are combinational functions of the pre-edge state: the PHT counter addressed by `predict_pc ^ GHR` and the current GHR itself. They must reflect the state *before* any edge-triggered update, even when `train_valid` is also asserted in the same cycle. The spec is explicit that a simultaneous training request does not affect the prediction output for that cycle; training only changes the PHT at the next posedge. So the checker should drive `predict_valid` and `train_valid` together, sample the outputs after the edge has settled, and confirm the outputs correspond to the old PHT entry and old GHR.

The next-state logic has a strict priority. If `train_valid && train_mispredicted`, the GHR is recovered to `{train_history[5:0], train_taken}` regardless of `predict_valid`. Otherwise, if `predict_valid`, the GHR shifts in the predicted direction. Otherwise it holds. Ordinary training (`train_valid && !train_mispredicted`) does not shift the GHR; it only updates the PHT counter at the trained index `train_pc ^ train_history`. This asymmetry is the most likely source of checker-visible bugs: an implementation that shifts on every training, or that lets a prediction shift override a misprediction recovery, will diverge on the very next cycle's `predict_history`.

The PHT update itself is edge-triggered and uses the saturating two-bit counter semantics: increment on taken, decrement on not-taken, clamped at 0 and 3. The trained index must be computed from `train_pc` and `train_history`, not from the current GHR or the prediction PC. A checker case where `train_pc != predict_pc` or `train_history != GHR` will catch index aliasing.

Worked example: GHR=0, PHT[0]=2, and in one cycle `predict_valid=1, predict_pc=0, train_valid=1, train_pc=0, train_history=0, train_taken=0, train_mispredicted=1`. Correct sampled outputs: `predict_taken=1` (counter 2, MSB set) and `predict_history=0` (old GHR). At the edge, PHT[0] becomes 1 and GHR becomes `{000000,0}=0`. A checker that sees `predict_taken=0` has caught premature training application; one that sees `predict_history=1` has caught a prediction-shift override of misprediction recovery. Both are decisive failures.

For mid-input changes: values driven after the sampling edge but before the next edge are carried into the next row's pre-edge state, so the checker must treat them as inputs to the next cycle's combinational evaluation, not as retroactive changes to the just-sampled outputs. Asynchronous active-high reset must clear the GHR and PHT independent of the clock, and the checker should verify that a reset asserted mid-cycle immediately forces the outputs to their reset values.
