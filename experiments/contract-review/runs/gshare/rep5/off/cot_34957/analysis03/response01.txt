The predictor's registered state consists of two elements: a 7-bit global branch history register (`ghr`) and a 128-entry PHT of 2-bit saturating counters. Both are updated only at the positive clock edge, except that asynchronous active-high `areset` overrides the next-state logic entirely. The prediction outputs are combinational functions of the current registered state and the current inputs: `predict_taken` is the MSB of the PHT entry indexed by `predict_pc ^ ghr`, and `predict_history` is the current `ghr`. Because these outputs are combinational, they reflect the pre-edge state during the cycle in which a prediction is requested; the effect of that prediction on `ghr` appears only after the edge. The checker must therefore sample outputs after the edge has settled, and must not assume reset forces `predict_taken` low — reset clears `ghr` and restores the PHT to its reset encoding, and the resulting `predict_taken` is whatever that cleared state implies for the applied `predict_pc`.

The next-state priority is: reset first, then training recovery, then prediction shift, then hold. When `areset` is high at the sampling point, the next `ghr` is zero and the PHT returns to its reset contents, regardless of `predict_valid`, `train_valid`, `train_mispredicted`, or the training-over-prediction precedence. Reset is level-sensitive and asynchronous: a mid-cycle assertion must clear state without waiting for a posedge, and a mid-cycle deassertion must not itself create a spurious update. Power-up without reset is unspecified, so the checker must assert `areset` before relying on any state.

When `train_valid` is high and `train_mispredicted` is high, the PHT entry indexed by `train_pc ^ train_history` is trained toward `train_taken` (saturating at 0 or 3), and `ghr` is recovered to the state immediately after the mispredicting branch — i.e., `{train_history[5:0], train_taken}`. When `train_valid` is high without a misprediction, only the PHT entry is updated; `ghr` is not recovered. When `predict_valid` is high and no training recovery occurs, `ghr` shifts in the predicted direction: `{ghr[5:0], predict_taken}`. If both a prediction and a misprediction-training occur in the same cycle, training recovery wins for `ghr`, because the predicted instruction will be flushed. If training and prediction touch the same PHT entry in the same cycle, the prediction reads the pre-edge PHT value; the training write is visible only after the edge.

Worked example: with `ghr = 7'h2A`, PHT entry at `predict_pc ^ ghr` holding `2'b11`, and in one cycle `predict_valid=1`, `train_valid=1`, `train_taken=0`, `train_mispredicted=1`, `areset=1`, the sampled outputs may still show the pre-reset registered state, but the next-edge state is the reset state: `ghr = 7'h00` and reset PHT contents. Reset overrides training recovery and the prediction shift alike. The checker should encode reset as the highest-priority next-state action, then training recovery, then prediction shift, then hold.
