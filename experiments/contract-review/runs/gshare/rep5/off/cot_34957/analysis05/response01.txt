The key distinguishing point for the checker is the separation of three independent decisions that the prose can easily blur together: (1) the PHT write, (2) the GHR next-state, and (3) the combinational prediction read. They share inputs but have different gating conditions, and a short test can expose a design that conflates them.

Consider a cycle with `areset=0`, `predict_valid=1`, `train_valid=1`, `train_mispredicted=0`, and `train_taken=1`, where `predict_pc ^ ghr` and `train_pc ^ train_history` resolve to the same PHT index. Here the prediction must output the pre-edge counter for that index, and the PHT write must occur after the edge using `train_taken`. The GHR must shift by the *predicted* direction (from the pre-edge PHT read), not by `train_taken`, because a non-mispredicted training does not touch GHR. A buggy implementation that lets training's `train_taken` leak into the GHR shift, or that suppresses the prediction shift whenever `train_valid` is high, will diverge here. This is a clean discriminator because the two directions can be chosen opposite.

A second useful discriminator is the same-index collision with a misprediction: `predict_valid=1`, `train_valid=1`, `train_mispredicted=1`. Prediction still reads the pre-edge PHT entry and reports pre-edge `ghr`; the PHT write still happens at the edge; but GHR recovery wins over the prediction shift, so next GHR = `{train_history[5:0], train_taken}` (append actual outcome to supplied history), and the prediction's shift is discarded. A design that applies prediction shift then recovery, or that skips the PHT write because recovery occurred, fails.

A third discriminator is reset level-sensitivity. With `areset=1` mid-cycle and no posedge, next state must be zeroed; a mid-cycle deassert must not itself trigger an update. The checker should assert reset before trusting state and should judge next-edge state rather than same-cycle outputs when reset is asserted, since sampled outputs may reflect pre-reset registers.

Worked example: `ghr=7'h2A`, `predict_pc=7'h15` so index `0x3F`; suppose that PHT entry is `2'b11` (predict taken). Same cycle: `train_valid=1`, `train_pc=7'h15`, `train_history=7'h2A`, `train_taken=0`, `train_mispredicted=0`, `areset=0`. Prediction outputs `predict_taken=1`, `predict_history=7'h2A`. After the edge: PHT[index] becomes `2'b10` (decrement from 11), and GHR shifts by predicted taken: `{ghr[5:0],1'b1}=7'h55`. If instead `train_mispredicted=1`, next GHR = `{2A[5:0],0}=7'h54`, and prediction's shift is discarded. These two cases differ only in `train_mispredicted`, making them a tight pair for the checker.
