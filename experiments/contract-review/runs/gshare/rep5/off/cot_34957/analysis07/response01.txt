The key timing distinction is between what the prediction interface observes *before* the sampling edge and what the state becomes *after* it. On any cycle where `predict_valid=1`, the outputs `predict_taken` and `predict_history` are combinational functions of the current PHT entry (indexed by `predict_pc` XOR current GHR) and the current GHR. They must reflect the pre-edge PHT contents and the pre-edge GHR, even if a training request in the same cycle targets the same PHT entry. Training's effect on the PHT is deferred to the next positive edge; the prediction therefore "sees" the old counter. This is the single most important rule for the checker: sample `predict_taken` and `predict_history` at the edge using the values that were valid *before* that edge settled, not the post-edge state.

The GHR update at the edge is governed by priority. If `train_valid=1` and `train_mispredicted=1`, recovery wins: the next GHR is `{train_history[5:0], train_taken}`. Otherwise, if `predict_valid=1`, the GHR shifts in the predicted direction: `{ghr[5:0], predict_taken}`. If neither applies, the GHR holds. Note that a non-mispredicted training request does *not* touch the GHR at all — it only updates the PHT counter. A common bug is to shift the GHR on every `train_valid`, or to shift by `train_taken` instead of `predict_taken` when only a prediction is present. The checker should verify the GHR after the edge against these three cases independently.

The PHT write is unconditional on `train_valid`, including when `train_mispredicted=1`. The write index is derived from `train_pc` XOR `train_history` — the *supplied* history, not the live GHR and not any shifted version. The counter saturates: increment on `train_taken=1`, decrement on `train_taken=0`, clamped to `2'b00`/`2'b11`. A design that skips the PHT write under misprediction, or that computes the index from a shifted GHR, will diverge from the reference on the very next prediction to that entry.

Worked discriminator: with `ghr=7'h2A`, `predict_pc=7'h15` (index `0x3F`), PHT[0x3F]=`2'b11`, and a same-cycle train with `train_pc=7'h15`, `train_history=7'h2A`, `train_taken=0`, `train_mispredicted=0`, the prediction must report `predict_taken=1`, `predict_history=7'h2A`; after the edge PHT[0x3F]=`2'b10` and GHR=`7'h55`. Flipping `train_mispredicted=1` leaves the prediction outputs identical and PHT still `2'b10`, but the next GHR becomes `7'h54`. A design that shifts by `train_taken` yields `7'h54` in the first case; one that suppresses the prediction shift under `train_valid` leaves `7'h2A`. Both fail.

Finally, `areset` is asynchronous active-high: the checker should assert it mid-cycle (between edges) and confirm the GHR and PHT clear without waiting for a clock edge, then release and resume normal sampling.
