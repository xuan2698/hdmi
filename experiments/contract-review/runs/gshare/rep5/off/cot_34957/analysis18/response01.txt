One likely implementation mistake is treating the global history register update as a simple "shift on every prediction" without honoring training priority. The spec says that when a mispredicted branch is trained in the same cycle as a prediction, training wins for the GHR: the register is recovered to `{train_history[5:0], train_taken}`, and the prediction's shift is discarded. A design that always shifts on `predict_valid` will corrupt the GHR after a flush.

Worked example: suppose the GHR is `0000000`, and in one cycle `predict_valid=1` with `predict_pc=0`, while `train_valid=1`, `train_mispredicted=1`, `train_history=0000000`, `train_taken=1`. The prediction must output `predict_history=0000000` and the old PHT value. At the edge, the GHR must become `0000001` from training recovery, not remain `0000000` from shifting in the predicted bit. The checker should verify this priority explicitly.
