The interface is narrow and fully specified: all data ports are 7 bits except the single-bit control and outcome signals. `predict_pc` and `train_pc` are 7-bit program counters, so the legal range is 0–127; the XOR with a 7-bit history also yields a 7-bit index, so the 128-entry PHT is exactly addressed with no masking or truncation. `train_history` and `predict_history` are 7-bit GHR values, range 0–127. `predict_taken` and `train_taken` are 1-bit directions. `train_mispredicted` is a 1-bit qualifier that both enables GHR recovery and selects the training-write behavior. `areset` is asynchronous active-high, so the checker must be able to assert it between edges and expect immediate clearing of GHR and PHT state, not merely at the next clock.

Boundary values matter for the checker. Index 0 and index 127 are both reachable: index 0 occurs when `pc ^ history == 0`, e.g. `pc=0, history=0` or `pc=127, history=127`; index 127 occurs when the XOR is all ones, e.g. `pc=127, history=0`. The checker should include at least one test at each extreme to catch accidental 6-bit truncation or off-by-one table sizing. Counter saturation is the other boundary: a 2-bit saturating counter must stop at `2'b11` on repeated taken training and at `2'b00` on repeated not-taken training. Training a counter already at `2'b11` with taken must leave it at `2'b11`; training `2'b00` with not-taken must leave it at `2'b00`. The prediction output is the MSB, so `2'b10` and `2'b11` predict taken, while `2'b00` and `2'b01` predict not-taken. A common bug is to treat the counter as signed or to wrap instead of saturate; the checker should drive three or more consecutive same-direction trainings and confirm the counter pins at the endpoint.

The GHR boundary is also worth probing. After reset the GHR is zero. A prediction shifts in `predict_taken`, so after seven taken predictions the GHR becomes `7'h7F`; an eighth taken prediction shifts out the oldest bit and keeps `7'h7F`. A not-taken prediction after `7'h7F` yields `7'h7E`. Training recovery sets the GHR to `{train_history[5:0], train_taken}`, which discards `train_history[6]`; the checker should verify that the recovered value uses the low six bits of the supplied history plus the actual outcome, not the full seven bits plus outcome (which would be 8 bits) and not the pre-edge GHR.

The same-cycle arbitration is the highest-value check. When `predict_valid` and a mispredicted `train_valid` coincide, the prediction outputs must reflect the pre-edge PHT entry and pre-edge GHR, while the edge commits the training PHT update and the training GHR recovery. The checker should sample outputs after the edge settles but compare them against values captured from the pre-edge state, and then confirm the next-cycle GHR equals the recovery value. A design that lets the prediction shift win, or that reads the PHT after the training write, will diverge here.

Worked example: after reset, train `pc=0, history=0` taken twice, reaching `2'b10`. Then assert `predict_valid=1, predict_pc=0` with `train_valid=1, train_pc=0, train_history=0, train_taken=1, train_mispredicted=1`. The prediction must output `predict_taken=1` and `predict_history=0`; at the edge the PHT becomes `2'b11` and the GHR becomes `7'h01`.
