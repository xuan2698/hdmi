The predictor's state consists of two elements: a 7-bit global history register (GHR) and a 128-entry pattern history table (PHT) of 2-bit saturating counters. Both are cleared asynchronously when `areset` is high, and reset dominates any coincident prediction or training request. The checker should treat reset as an immediate state-clearing event, not an edge-triggered one: after asserting `areset` mid-cycle, GHR and every PHT entry must read as zero without waiting for a clock edge, and any `predict_valid`/`train_valid` asserted in that same cycle must have no effect on state.

On a normal sampled edge with `predict_valid=1` and no reset, the predictor computes `index = predict_pc ^ GHR` (7-bit XOR), reads `PHT[index]`, and drives `predict_taken = PHT[index][1]` (MSB of the 2-bit counter) and `predict_history = GHR` (the pre-update value). The GHR then shifts at the next positive edge: `GHR <= {GHR[5:0], predict_taken}`. Note the prediction output reflects the PHT state *before* any same-cycle training write, since training only commits at the edge. The checker must sample outputs after the edge settles but must reason that the PHT read used the pre-edge contents.

On a sampled edge with `train_valid=1`, the predictor computes the same index from `train_pc ^ train_history` and updates that PHT entry at the next edge according to `train_taken`: increment toward `2'b11` on taken, decrement toward `2'b00` on not-taken, saturating. If `train_mispredicted=1`, the GHR is also recovered at the edge to `{train_history[5:0], train_taken}` — i.e., the history immediately after the mispredicting branch. If `train_mispredicted=0`, the GHR is not modified by training.

The critical arbitration rule: when training (with misprediction) and prediction occur in the same cycle, training wins for the GHR. The prediction still produces its outputs (using pre-edge PHT and pre-edge GHR), but the GHR update at the edge is the training recovery value, not the prediction shift. The checker should verify this by driving both interfaces simultaneously with a mispredicted training and confirming the next-cycle GHR equals the training recovery value, while the prediction outputs in that cycle matched the pre-edge state.

A useful discriminator: after reset, train PHT entry for `pc=0, history=0` with `train_taken=1` twice to reach `2'b10`. Then in one cycle assert `predict_valid=1, predict_pc=0` (GHR=0, index 0) together with `train_valid=1, train_pc=0, train_history=0, train_taken=1, train_mispredicted=1`. The prediction must output `predict_taken=1` (pre-edge `2'b10`) and `predict_history=0`; at the edge the PHT becomes `2'b11` and GHR becomes `{6'b0,1}=7'h01`. A design that lets the prediction shift override the training recovery, or that reads the post-training PHT, fails.
