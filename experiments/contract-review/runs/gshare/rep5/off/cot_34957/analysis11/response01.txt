The most useful distinguishing point for the final checker is the interaction between the prediction interface and the training interface in the same cycle, especially when the training request is a misprediction. The public spec is explicit that training takes precedence over prediction for the GHR update, and that a prediction must observe the PHT state before any training write in that cycle. A checker that only exercises prediction and training in separate cycles will miss the two most common implementation bugs: (1) letting the prediction shift update the GHR when a mispredicted training also occurs, and (2) reading the PHT after the training write has been applied.

A short worked example illustrates the required behavior. After reset, the GHR is zero and all PHT entries are presumably `2'b00` (or some reset value; the spec does not pin the reset value, so the checker should either avoid depending on it or first train the entry to a known state). Suppose we first train `pc=0, history=0` taken twice. The first training moves the counter from its reset value toward taken; the second training moves it further. If the reset value is `2'b00`, after two taken trainings the counter is `2'b10`, which predicts taken. Now assert `predict_valid=1, predict_pc=0` together with `train_valid=1, train_pc=0, train_history=0, train_taken=1, train_mispredicted=1`. The prediction must output `predict_taken=1` because the PHT entry is `2'b10` before the edge, and `predict_history=0` because the GHR is zero before the edge. At the positive edge, the training request updates the PHT entry to `2'b11` and recovers the GHR to `{train_history[5:0], train_taken} = {6'b000000, 1'b1} = 7'h01`. The next-cycle GHR must be `7'h01`, not `7'h00` (prediction shift) and not `7'h02` (using the full history plus outcome). The next prediction for `pc=0` should see the updated PHT entry `2'b11` and predict taken, and its `predict_history` should be `7'h01`.

The checker should also probe saturation independently. Drive three or more consecutive taken trainings to the same PHT entry and confirm the counter pins at `2'b11`; then drive three or more consecutive not-taken trainings and confirm it pins at `2'b00`. A wrap-around or signed-counter bug will diverge on the third same-direction training. Similarly, probe the GHR boundary: seven taken predictions should produce `7'h7F`, an eighth taken prediction should keep `7'h7F`, and a not-taken prediction after `7'h7F` should yield `7'h7E`. These checks are cheap and catch shift-width and masking errors.

Finally, the checker should sample outputs after the edge settles but compare them against pre-edge state. The prediction outputs are combinational functions of the pre-edge PHT entry and pre-edge GHR; the training effects are committed only at the edge. A design that updates the PHT or GHR before the prediction is sampled will produce a different `predict_taken` or `predict_history` in the same-cycle case. The worked example above is the minimal distinguishing test: it forces the prediction to see `2'b10` and `7'h00` while the edge commits `2'b11` and `7'h01`.
