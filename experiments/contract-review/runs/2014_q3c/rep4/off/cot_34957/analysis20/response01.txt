**Reset and power-up behavior.** The public specification gives no explicit reset input, no reset value, and no power-up state. The state-assigned table defines behavior only for the eight encoded present states 000 through 111, and only five of those rows are listed (000, 001, 010, 011, 100). The remaining encodings 101, 110, and 111 have no specified next-state or output behavior. Consequently, the checker should not assume any particular initial value of `y` at time zero, nor should it assume that the design reaches a legal state after power-up without an external stimulus. Any verification of reset behavior would require a reset signal or initialization convention that the public spec does not provide; absent that, the checker should treat the first sampled row as authoritative and drive `y` and `x` explicitly before the first sampled edge rather than relying on the design's power-up state.

This matters for the final Python checker in a concrete way: the checker must not compare `Y[0]` or `z` on any cycle before it has itself established a known present state `y`. If the checker samples immediately after power-up without first driving a legal `y`, the design's outputs are unspecified by the public table, and a mismatch would be a checker artifact rather than a design failure. The safe pattern is to drive the row's `y` and `x` before the sampled edge, capture the pre-edge `x`, compute the expected `Y[0]` from the captured `x` and the driven `y`, compute `z` from `y` alone, and only then compare after the edge settles. Rows whose `y` is one of the unspecified encodings (101, 110, 111) should be excluded from expectation checking, since the table assigns them no next state or output.

**Worked example.** Drive `y=011`, `x=0` before the positive edge. The table gives next state 001, so expected `Y[0]=1`, and `z=1`. After the edge settles, compare those values. If `x` later changes to 1 in the mid-cycle window, that is setup for the next row and must not retroactively change the expectation for the 011 row; a checker that re-reads `x` after the edge would wrongly expect next state 010 and `Y[0]=0`.

**Useful point for the final checker.** Capture `x` before the sampled edge and bind it to that row; use `y` alone for `z`; skip expectation checks for unlisted present-state encodings and for any pre-initialization cycle.
